# Claude Code Research Instructions
## Talkerstein | Canadian SME Business Knowledge Base

### Objective

Research and build a comprehensive, evidence-based Markdown knowledge base about the challenges, questions, needs, and opportunities of Canadian small and medium-sized business owners.

This knowledge base will be consumed by the Gemini API as its primary reference material for generating:

- SEO articles and blog posts
- Business guides and educational content
- Answers to Canadian business owners' questions
- Industry-specific recommendations
- Marketing and technology insights
- AI, automation, and digital transformation content

The research must be specific to Canada, particularly the realities of operating an SME in Toronto and Ontario.

Do not produce generic entrepreneurship content.

---

# 1. Research Pillars

Organize all research into these four pillars.

Research:

- Common misconceptions

- Relevant tools and platforms

# 2. Canadian Market Research

This section is mandatory across all four pillars.

Research the realities of Canadian SMEs, with particular attention to:

### Geography
- Canada-wide business conditions
- Ontario-specific regulations and business practices
- Toronto and Greater Toronto Area market conditions
- Provincial differences where relevant

### Business Environment
- Canadian business terminology
- Canadian consumer behaviour
- Local competition
- Small business operating costs
- Labour and hiring considerations
- Payment preferences and payment infrastructure
- Common software and platforms used by Canadian businesses
- Canadian business tax considerations where relevant

### Legal and Regulatory Context

Research applicable requirements and distinguish between federal, provincial, and municipal rules.

Pay particular attention to:
- CRA requirements
- GST/HST
- PIPEDA
- Ontario's privacy and consumer protection requirements
- CASL (Canadian Anti-Spam Legislation)
- AODA and website accessibility considerations
- Employment standards
- Consumer protection
- Data residency and data handling considerations

Do not give definitive legal or tax advice. Explain when professional advice is necessary.

# 3. Search Demand and Keyword Research

For each pillar, identify the questions and search terms Canadian business owners are likely to use.

Use available research sources and SEO tools where accessible.

Prioritize:
- Google search intent
- Commercial investigation
- Problem-aware searches
- Solution-aware searches
- Transactional searches
- Long-tail keywords
- Question-based searches
- Local search queries

For each keyword, record:

| Field | Description |
|---|---|
| Keyword | Actual search phrase |
| Search intent | Informational, commercial, transactional, navigational |
| Audience | Who is searching |
| Pain point | Problem behind the search |
| Business opportunity | Relevant Talkerstein service |
| Search volume | Monthly volume, if verifiable |
| Competition | Difficulty or competition, if available |
| Location | Canada, Ontario, Toronto, or other |

Important:
- Never invent search volumes.
- Clearly distinguish measured data from estimates.
- Do not claim a topic is the most searched without supporting evidence.
- Identify gaps where reliable keyword data is unavailable.
- Prioritize commercially valuable keywords, not just high-volume keywords.

# 4. Business Owner Questions Database

Build a structured database of questions Canadian business owners ask.

For each pillar, collect at least 50 meaningful questions.

Organize them by:

1. Awareness: The owner recognizes a problem.
2. Consideration: The owner is researching solutions.
3. Decision: The owner is comparing providers or ready to invest.
4. Implementation: The owner needs help using or improving a solution.

For each question, provide:

- Question
- Underlying business problem
- Search intent
- Relevant industry
- Recommended answer direction
- Relevant tools or solutions
- Related Talkerstein service
- Supporting sources

Questions must sound natural, as actual business owners would phrase them.

Avoid creating repetitive variations of the same question.

# 7. Source and Research Standards

Use authoritative sources first.

### Preferred sources

Government:
- Statistics Canada
- Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada
- Canada Revenue Agency
- Government of Ontario
- City of Toronto
- Office of the Privacy Commissioner of Canada
- Canadian Centre for Cyber Security

Business research:
- Canadian Federation of Independent Business (CFIB)
- Business Development Bank of Canada (BDC)
- Canadian Chamber of Commerce
- Ontario Chamber of Commerce
- Deloitte Canada
- PwC Canada
- KPMG Canada

Technology:
- Google Search Central
- Google Think
- Shopify
- Microsoft
- HubSpot
- Salesforce
- Official product documentation

Community research:
- Reddit
- Canadian small business communities
- Canadian entrepreneur forums
- Industry-specific discussions

Reddit and community discussions can provide qualitative insights, but do not treat individual opinions as verified facts.

### Source requirements

Every factual claim should have a traceable source.

For every source, record:
- Title
- Publisher
- Publication date
- URL
- Date accessed
- Relevant findings
- Reliability level

Prioritize research published within the last 2–3 years for market trends and technology adoption.

Use older sources only when they remain relevant.

Flag conflicting or outdated information.

# 9. Gemini API Optimization

The final output is intended to be used as a knowledge source for Gemini API.

Therefore:

- Use clean Markdown.
- Use descriptive headings.
- Keep individual files focused on one subject.
- Avoid unnecessarily long paragraphs.
- Use tables for structured comparisons.
- Define technical terminology.
- Include explicit geographic context.
- Include publication dates for time-sensitive information.
- Keep source citations close to the relevant claims.
- Avoid duplicated information across files.
- Make relationships between topics explicit.
- Separate verified facts, estimates, opinions, and recommendations.

Every research document should begin with YAML frontmatter:

```yaml
---
title: "Document Title"
pillar: "Getting More Customers"
geography:
  - Canada
  - Ontario
  - Toronto
last_updated: "YYYY-MM-DD"
source_count: 0
document_type: "research"
---
```

Use consistent terminology across all documents.

The knowledge base must be understandable without relying on the original research session.

# 10. Final Quality Requirements

Before completing the project:

1. Verify that all four pillars have sufficient research.
2. Check that Canadian context is consistently applied.
3. Remove duplicate questions and redundant information.
4. Verify important statistics against their original sources.
5. Identify outdated information.
6. Ensure every factual claim has a source.
7. Clearly label information that cannot be verified.
8. Check that all internal references and Markdown links work.
9. Ensure the documents are suitable for retrieval and context injection through Gemini API.
10. Update README.md with an index of all research documents and a summary of the knowledge base.

### Research Philosophy

The objective is not to create a collection of generic business articles.

The objective is to understand how Canadian business owners think, what problems they encounter, what questions they ask, what solutions they consider, and where technology can genuinely improve their businesses.

Research should be practical, commercially relevant, evidence-based, and grounded in the Canadian business environment.

**Think like a Canadian SME business researcher, not a content writer.**
