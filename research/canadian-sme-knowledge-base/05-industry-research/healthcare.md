# Healthcare Clinics in Ontario: Growth, Operations, Digital and AI

This file covers private and mixed-funding clinics in Toronto and Ontario: physiotherapy, chiropractic, dental, psychotherapy and counselling, massage, medical aesthetics, optometry and family or walk-in medical clinics. Health privacy (PHIPA) is in [privacy-and-data-protection.md](../06-canadian-regulations/privacy-and-data-protection.md) (planned).

**This is general information, not legal or regulatory advice.** Each regulated health profession in Ontario has its own college with its own advertising rules; check the college for the profession before publishing marketing.

## Advertising rules for regulated health professionals in Ontario

Health colleges restrict testimonials, superlatives and comparative claims far more than general advertising law does.

**Psychotherapists (CRPO), verified.** From CRPO's guidance on [advertising, testimonials, endorsements and community voting awards](https://crpo.ca/resource-articles/advertising-testimonials-endorsements-and-community-voting-awards/), based on Professional Practice Standard 6.2:

- Using a testimonial from a client, former client or other person in advertising is professional misconduct.
- Community voting awards and star ratings can count as testimonials; "Voted Best ..." claims with superlatives are inappropriate.
- Clients may post reviews only if the registrant does not request them or influence which are published. Linking advertising directly to third-party review sites may be inappropriate. Do not ask clients to vote in "best of" contests.
- Avoid promising results, superlatives, comparisons and appeals to fear.

**Physicians (CPSO) and dentists (RCDSO). Unverified:** the official pages could not be opened on 2026-11-12. Secondary sources describe the CPSO Advertising policy (approved December 2020) as prohibiting testimonials, restricting before-and-after images and making physicians responsible for ads prepared by third parties such as marketing agencies. Confirm directly with the CPSO and RCDSO before publishing reviews, before-and-after galleries or awards.

**Recommendation for agencies:** assume no testimonials, no review widgets, no "best" or comparative claims and no before/after galleries for a regulated health clinic until the specific college's current rules are checked. The clinic, not the agency, carries the professional-misconduct risk.

## Clinic business models and acquisition challenges

| Model | Funding | Booking pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Physio, chiro, massage, psychotherapy | Mostly private pay and extended health insurance | Series of visits; rebooking matters |
| Dental | Private pay, insurance, public dental programs | Recall cycles (cleanings) |
| Family / walk-in medical | OHIP-funded | Capacity-limited; less marketing need |
| Medical aesthetics | Private pay | Consult-led, high ticket |

**Acquisition challenges (opinion):**

- Advertising rules remove the usual review- and testimonial-led tactics, so content, credentials and search visibility do more work.
- Patients search by condition and insurance ("physio near me direct billing").
- No-shows and late cancellations waste capacity.
- Health care is a priority area for AI: StatCan found planned AI use in health care and social assistance doubled from 11.4% to 23.2% between Q3 2024 and Q3 2025 ([expected use of AI, Q3 2025](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2025011-eng.htm)).

## Operational bottlenecks in clinics

**Opinion, based on common practice; not measured:** phone-based booking and front-desk overload; intake forms on paper and re-typed; insurance direct billing and claim follow-up; charting time after hours; recall and rebooking not tracked; reminders sent by hand.

## Website features and digital presence for clinics

| Feature | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Online booking by practitioner, service and location | Cuts phone load |
| Condition and service pages written by or reviewed by clinicians | Search visibility within advertising rules |
| Practitioner bios with college registration | Trust; regulatory accuracy |
| Insurance, direct billing and fees page | Pre-qualifies patients |
| Secure digital intake forms | Avoid collecting health information through ordinary email forms (privacy, see planned privacy file) |
| Accessibility and clear location/transit info | AODA, see [digital-accessibility.md](../06-canadian-regulations/digital-accessibility.md) |

## Automation and AI use cases for clinics

**Automations (recommendations):** booking confirmations and reminders by SMS/email; waitlist fill for cancellations; recall reminders (dental cleanings, physio plan check-ins); digital intake that flows into the clinic management system.

**AI use cases (recommendations), with health privacy controls:** AI scribes to draft clinical notes for practitioner review; FAQ assistants for hours, services, insurance and booking (no clinical advice); drafting patient education content for clinician review. Personal health information should not be sent to AI tools without a privacy assessment and appropriate agreements (see [ai-privacy-and-governance.md](../04-ai-for-business/ai-privacy-and-governance.md), planned).

**Examples of software (not an endorsement; usage share not verified):** Jane App, Cliniko and similar clinic management systems; dental practice software such as ABELDent or Dentrix; OSCAR Pro, TELUS PS Suite or Accuro EMRs for medical clinics.

**Custom development opportunities (recommendation):** location/practitioner finder integrated with the booking system; multi-clinic landing page system; secure intake integrations; reporting dashboards across locations. Avoid custom booking engines where the clinic system already provides one.
