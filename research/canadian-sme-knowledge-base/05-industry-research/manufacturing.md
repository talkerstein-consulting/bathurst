# Manufacturing, Wholesale and Distribution in Ontario: Growth, Operations, Digital and AI

This file covers small and mid-sized manufacturers, contract and job shops, and wholesale and distribution businesses in Ontario. B2B services are in [professional-services.md](professional-services.md); B2C online selling is in [retail-and-ecommerce.md](retail-and-ecommerce.md).

## Ontario manufacturing activity in 2026

From Statistics Canada's Monthly Survey of Manufacturing:

- **May 2026:** Ontario sales rose 2.0% to $32.6 billion, the fourth straight monthly gain and the highest since September 2023. National sales reached a record $78.1 billion ([The Daily, 2026-07-15](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260715/dq260715a-eng.htm)).
- **February 2026:** Ontario sales rose in 14 of 21 subsectors, led by transportation equipment (+24.7% to $6.9 billion) and machinery (+21.7% to $2.2 billion). National capacity utilization was 77.1% ([The Daily, 2026-04-15](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260415/dq260415a-eng.htm)).

Ontario's figures swing with the auto sector, so suppliers to transportation equipment face volatile demand.

## Business models in manufacturing and distribution

| Model | Revenue | How buyers find them |
|---|---|---|
| Contract / job shop (machining, fabrication, packaging) | Quotes per job or run | RFQs, referrals, supplier directories, search |
| Own-brand product manufacturer | Wholesale to retailers and distributors, some direct-to-consumer | Trade shows, distributors, online catalogues |
| Food and beverage producer | Retail listings, foodservice, DTC | Buyers, distributors, retail programs |
| Wholesaler / distributor | Margin on resale; account pricing | Sales reps, dealer portals, repeat orders |

## Customer acquisition challenges for manufacturers and distributors

**Opinion, based on common practice; not measured:**

- Sales depend on a few large accounts and the owner's network.
- Websites describe the company, not capabilities (materials, tolerances, certifications, minimums, lead times), so engineers and buyers cannot qualify the shop.
- RFQs arrive by email with drawings and are answered slowly.
- Distributors and dealers lack self-serve pricing, stock and reorder.

For B2B buying behaviour (professional contacts and online search both used by 54% of owners), see [customer-acquisition.md](../01-getting-more-customers/customer-acquisition.md).

## Operational bottlenecks in manufacturing and wholesale

**Opinion:** quoting (estimating material, machine time and margin) by spreadsheet; inventory counts out of sync between ERP, spreadsheets and the floor; manual order entry from emailed POs; production scheduling on whiteboards; quality and traceability records on paper; slow invoicing and collections.

## Digital presence and website features for manufacturers

| Feature | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Capabilities pages (processes, materials, tolerances, equipment list) | Lets buyers qualify you without a call |
| Certifications and industries served | Trust for procurement |
| RFQ form with secure drawing upload (STEP, DWG, PDF) | Captures complete requests |
| Product catalogue with spec sheets and downloadable CAD/data | For own-brand makers |
| B2B dealer portal: account pricing, stock, reorder, order history | For distributors (see [ecommerce-and-payments.md](../08-software-directory/ecommerce-and-payments.md)) |
| Careers page | Skilled-labour recruitment (opinion) |

## Automation and AI use cases in manufacturing and distribution

StatCan's AI surveys show falling planned adoption in these sectors: planned use over the next 12 months fell from 13.1% to 7.2% in manufacturing and from 12.2% to 7.8% in wholesale between Q3 2024 and Q3 2025 ([expected use of AI, Q3 2025](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2025011-eng.htm)). In the Q2 2025 survey, 16.2% of manufacturers and 20.4% of wholesalers planned to adopt AI software ([AI use, Q2 2025](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2025008-eng.htm)); the two measures differ, so compare carefully.

**Automations (recommendations):** emailed PO to ERP order entry; low-stock alerts and reorder suggestions; order status emails; quote follow-up; automatic invoices on shipment.

**AI use cases (recommendations):** extracting line items from POs and RFQ emails; first-pass quote drafts from drawings and past jobs (reviewed by an estimator); product description and spec sheet writing; answering stock and order-status questions through a portal assistant; demand forecasting from order history.

## Common software and custom development in manufacturing and distribution

**Examples (not an endorsement; usage share not verified):** ERPs such as Odoo, Acumatica, NetSuite or Microsoft Dynamics Business Central; inventory tools such as Cin7 or inFlow; Shopify B2B or similar for dealer ordering; QuickBooks Online for smaller shops.

**Custom development opportunities (recommendation):** RFQ intake portal connected to the estimating spreadsheet or ERP; dealer/reorder portal integrated with inventory; product configurator that outputs a quote; traceability dashboards. Use off-the-shelf ERP modules first where they fit.
