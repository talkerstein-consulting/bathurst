# The Canadian, Ontario and Toronto SME Landscape

This file sets out the basic facts about small and medium-sized enterprises (SMEs) in Canada, Ontario and Toronto: how many there are, how "small" is defined, what they pay for labour, how customers pay them, and what owners say is holding them back in late 2026. Pillar-specific detail lives in the pillar folders, for example [01-getting-more-customers/pillar-overview.md](01-getting-more-customers/pillar-overview.md). Rules and regulations are in [06-canadian-regulations/federal-requirements.md](06-canadian-regulations/federal-requirements.md) and [06-canadian-regulations/ontario-requirements.md](06-canadian-regulations/ontario-requirements.md).

## How Canada defines a small or medium-sized business

Innovation, Science and Economic Development Canada (ISED) uses employee counts for employer businesses ([Key Small Business Statistics 2024](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/sme-research-statistics/en/key-small-business-statistics/key-small-business-statistics-2024), data as of December 2023):

| Size | Paid employees |
|---|---|
| Small | 1 to 99 |
| Medium | 100 to 499 |
| Large | 500 or more |

Notes:

- These counts cover **employer businesses** only. Self-employed people with no paid staff are not in these totals.
- Lenders, grant programs and tax rules use their own definitions (often revenue-based). Check the definition each program uses before assuming a business qualifies.
- **Opinion:** most Talkerstein clients (trades, clinics, professional firms, local retail) fall in the small category.

## How many SMEs there are in Canada and Ontario

From ISED's [Key Small Business Statistics 2024](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/sme-research-statistics/en/key-small-business-statistics/key-small-business-statistics-2024) (December 2023 data):

| Measure | Canada | Ontario |
|---|---|---|
| Employer businesses | 1,095,251 | 415,653 |
| Small (1 to 99 employees) | 1,074,939 (98.1%) | 407,428 |
| Medium (100 to 499) | 16,966 (1.5%) | 6,728 |
| Large (500+) | 3,346 (0.3%) | 1,497 |

- Small businesses employed about 5.8 million people, or 46.5% of the private-sector workforce, in 2023. SMEs together employed 63.7%.
- Ontario has about 38% of Canada's employer businesses (calculated from the table) and 39.6% of private-sector employment.

**Flag:** these figures describe December 2023. Check for a newer ISED edition before quoting them.

## Business openings and closures in Ontario

CFIB reported in April 2026 that more businesses closed than opened in Ontario and Canada for six consecutive quarters ([More businesses have been closing than opening in Ontario and Canada](https://www.cfib-fcei.ca/en/media/more-businesses-have-been-closing-than-opening-in-ontario-and-canada-were-in-an-entrepreneurial-drought), 2026-04-15):

- Ontario's business exit rate reached 6.7% in Q2 2025 (Canada 5.6%).
- Ontario's entry rate fell to 5.1% in Q4 2025 (Canada 4.8%).
- 55% of Ontario and Canadian small business owners said they would not recommend starting a business right now.
- CFIB attributes this to the tax burden, red tape, high costs, an unpredictable economy, trade uncertainty and labour challenges.

**Opinion:** many owners are in defensive mode. Proposals framed around protecting revenue, saving time and cutting waste will likely land better than growth-at-any-cost pitches.

## What Canadian business owners say is holding them back in late 2026

Three recent sources agree that weak demand, costs and labour are the main problems.

**CFIB Business Barometer, October 2026** ([Business Barometer results](https://www.cfib-fcei.ca/en/research/business-barometer)):

- Long-term optimism index 50.0 and short-term index 45.3, both flat. The four largest provinces, including Ontario, sit near the 50-point breakeven.
- Weak customer demand limits 50% of SMEs; a shortage of skilled labour limits 43%.
- Fuel costs are the top cost constraint, elevated since an April 2026 peak.
- About 70% of small firms expect to pass on only a very small part of higher costs (about 21% on average).

**Statistics Canada, Canadian Survey on Business Conditions, Q3 2026** ([release](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260831/dq260831a-eng.htm)):

- 41.6% of businesses expect inflation to be an obstacle in the next three months; 25.2% expect recruiting skilled employees to be one.
- Only 14.5% expect sales to rise, while 13.9% expect them to fall.

**BDC State of Entrepreneurship Report 2025** ([report page](https://www.bdc.ca/en/about/analysis-research/bdc-state-entrepreneurship-report-2025), October 2025) names rising costs, declining demand and increased competition as the main headwinds, yet 92% of owners would choose entrepreneurship again.

## Labour costs and the hiring market in Ontario

- **Minimum wage:** Ontario's general minimum wage is $17.95 an hour from October 1, 2026. The student rate (under 18, 28 hours a week or less during the school year) is $16.90 ([Your guide to the Employment Standards Act: minimum wage](https://www.ontario.ca/document/your-guide-employment-standards-act-0/minimum-wage)).
- **Unemployment:** In October 2026 the national unemployment rate was 6.7% and Ontario's was 7.3%, both up on September ([Labour Force Survey, October 2026](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/daily-quotidien/261106/dq261106a-eng.htm)).
- **Wages:** Average hourly wages rose 2.2% year over year to $37.89 in October 2026 (not seasonally adjusted, same source).

**Unverified:** a Toronto CMA unemployment rate for October 2026 was not found in the sources opened. Use Statistics Canada's CMA tables if a Toronto figure is needed.

**Opinion:** rising unemployment alongside a reported skilled-labour shortage suggests a skills mismatch rather than a lack of applicants. Employment rules and payroll are covered in [06-canadian-regulations/ontario-requirements.md](06-canadian-regulations/ontario-requirements.md) and [06-canadian-regulations/federal-requirements.md](06-canadian-regulations/federal-requirements.md).

## How Canadians pay businesses

Payments Canada's 2025 report covers 22.5 billion retail transactions worth $12.2 trillion in 2024 ([Canada reaches $12.2 trillion in payment transactions in 2024](https://www.payments.ca/insights/research/canada-reaches-122-trillion-payment-transactions-2024-credit-cards-accounting-1-3), published 2025-10-02):

| Method (2024) | Share of transactions | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Credit card | 33% | Average $105; volume up 6% |
| Debit card | 30% | |
| Cash | 11% | Average $27; 48% of Canadians still use it frequently |
| Online transfers | 7% | Volume up 175% over five years |
| Contactless (any card or device) | 58% | About 13 billion payments |

**Recommendations:**

- Accept tap payments on both credit and debit; contactless is now the majority of transactions.
- Keep a cash option for small-ticket, older or walk-in customers.
- For larger invoices, offer bank transfer (for example Interac e-Transfer or EFT) to avoid card fees.
- Check card fees and surcharging rules with your processor before passing costs to customers.

## Toronto and GTA market conditions

- Toronto is a dense, competitive market; the City's 2025 Employment Survey figures are summarized in [01-getting-more-customers/pillar-overview.md](01-getting-more-customers/pillar-overview.md).
- Ontario's unemployment rate (7.3% in October 2026) is above the national rate (6.7%) ([Labour Force Survey](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/daily-quotidien/261106/dq261106a-eng.htm)).
- With weak demand the top constraint nationally ([CFIB Business Barometer](https://www.cfib-fcei.ca/en/research/business-barometer)), GTA businesses are competing harder for the same customers.

**Opinion:** in the GTA, high rents and wages make customer acquisition cost and staff time the two numbers most worth tracking. No Toronto-specific rent survey was reviewed for this file.
