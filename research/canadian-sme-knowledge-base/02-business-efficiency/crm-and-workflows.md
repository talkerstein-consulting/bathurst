# CRM Systems and Sales Workflows for Canadian Small Businesses

This file covers what a CRM (customer relationship management software) does for a small business, how to choose one, Canadian considerations such as data storage and French support, costs, and how to roll it out so staff actually use it. Lead response and qualification are in [../01-getting-more-customers/lead-generation.md](../01-getting-more-customers/lead-generation.md).

## What a CRM does for a small business

A CRM keeps every customer and lead in one place with their history, status and next step. BDC describes a CRM as partly a business strategy, not just software: it works best when it matches how you serve customers and your team uses it ([BDC: CRM software free and low-cost options](https://www.bdc.ca/en/articles-tools/technology/free-low-cost-applications/crm-solutions-free-low-cost-options), updated 2022-10-04, page also marked revised November 2026).

BDC warns against using a CRM "in a silo" and says its value extends beyond sales and marketing to other departments ([BDC: CRM system: How it can help grow your business](https://www.bdc.ca/en/articles-tools/marketing-sales-export/sales/how-use-technology-boost-sales), undated).

**Recommendation:** Signs a business needs a CRM: leads are tracked in an inbox or spreadsheet, nobody knows who followed up, quotes are lost, or customer history leaves when an employee does.

## How to choose a CRM: BDC's criteria

From BDC's CRM guidance (links above):

1. **Define requirements first, including integrations:** which systems the CRM must connect to (email, accounting, website forms) and which departments need it.
2. **Consider your industry and work structure** to find an organizational, financial and functional fit.
3. **Right-size it:** pick the smallest system that meets the requirements.
4. **Use free trials** before committing.
5. **Check tools you already use:** some CRMs are built for Google Workspace, Microsoft Outlook or Sage accounting.
6. **Expect tier limits:** BDC notes AI tools and advanced automation are typically in higher-priced plans.

## What a small business CRM costs

Verified prices (2026-11-12):

- **HubSpot** ([HubSpot CRM pricing](https://www.hubspot.com/pricing/crm)), USD: Free for up to 2 users, 1,000 contacts, 2,000 branded marketing emails a month and 10 dashboards. Starter is US$20/seat/month standard, with new-customer pricing of US$7 (annual upfront) or US$10 (monthly). Starter removes HubSpot branding and adds support.
- **Zoho CRM:** Canadian-dollar prices could not be confirmed (the CAD pricing URL returned 404 on 2026-11-12). **Unverified**.
- **Jobber** (CRM plus scheduling and invoicing for home services): Core US$29/month annual, 1 user ([Jobber pricing](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/)).

**Recommendation:** Budget for the plan you will need in 12 months, not the free tier. Per-seat pricing grows with the team, and features like automation and AI usually sit in paid tiers (BDC, above).

## Building a simple sales pipeline and workflow

**Recommendation** (Talkerstein's starting template for service businesses):

| Stage | Exit rule | Automation |
|---|---|---|
| New enquiry | Contacted | Form creates contact and alerts owner |
| Contacted | Need confirmed | Task reminder if no contact in 1 business day |
| Quote sent | Accepted or declined | Follow-up reminder after 3 and 7 days |
| Won | Job booked | Create job/project, send welcome email |
| Lost | Reason recorded | Tag reason for reporting |

Keep stages to five or six. Record "How did you hear about us?" at the first stage to measure marketing (see [../01-getting-more-customers/marketing-and-advertising.md](../01-getting-more-customers/marketing-and-advertising.md)). Follow-up emails to leads can be commercial electronic messages under CASL; see [../06-canadian-regulations/federal-requirements.md](../06-canadian-regulations/federal-requirements.md).

## Rolling out a CRM so the team actually uses it

BDC's implementation steps ([BDC: CRM system](https://www.bdc.ca/en/articles-tools/marketing-sales-export/sales/how-use-technology-boost-sales)):

1. Map end-to-end processes and where the CRM connects to each department's tools.
2. Appoint a digitally savvy project manager for configuration, training and a staged rollout tested on sample data.
3. Set up two-way integrations.
4. Clean contact lists before importing.
5. Train all relevant staff, including on integration points outside their own area.
6. After launch, collect feedback, adjust and revisit regularly.

## Common CRM mistakes

Based on BDC's guidance above, plus **Opinion** from implementation experience where marked:

- Importing outdated or duplicate data (BDC: clean first).
- Using the CRM only for sales (BDC: avoid silos).
- Treating launch as the finish line (BDC: revisit regularly).
- **Opinion:** Too many required fields, so staff stop entering data.
- **Opinion:** No single owner of CRM data quality.
- **Opinion:** Buying an enterprise CRM for a five-person team.
