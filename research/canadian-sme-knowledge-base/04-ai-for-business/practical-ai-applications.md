# Practical AI Applications for Canadian Small Businesses

This file covers where AI is useful to a Canadian SME today, task by task, and where it is not. Workflow design, costs and ROI are in [ai-automation.md](ai-automation.md); connecting AI to other software is in [ai-agents-and-integrations.md](ai-agents-and-integrations.md); privacy rules are in [ai-privacy-and-governance.md](ai-privacy-and-governance.md).

Statements with links are verified facts. **Recommendation** and **Opinion** labels mark advice and judgement, not facts.

## What Canadian businesses actually use AI for

Among Canadian businesses that used AI in the 12 months before Q2 2026, the most common applications were ([Statistics Canada, AI use Q2 2026](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2026010-eng.htm)):

| Application | Share of AI-using businesses, Q2 2026 | Q2 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Data analytics | 36.6% | 26.4% |
| Text analytics | 34.5% | 35.7% |
| Virtual agents or chatbots | 28.2% | 24.8% |
| Natural language processing | 27.0% | 23.1% |
| Large language models | 24.8% | 19.1% |
| Speech or voice recognition | 20.6% | 20.0% |
| Marketing automation | 19.8% | 23.1% |
| Recommendation systems | 17.9% | 14.0% |
| Decision-making systems | 13.7% | 5.7% |

Among workers, 35.9% used generative AI tools at work in the year to March 2026. Most users (63.5%) used it for some but not most tasks; 31.4% used it daily ([Statistics Canada](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260730/dq260730b-eng.htm)).

**Opinion:** For most SMEs the realistic starting point is AI inside tools they already pay for (email, office suite, CRM, accounting) plus a general assistant, not a custom model.

## AI customer service and website chatbots

**What it does:** answers routine questions (hours, services, booking, order status) from the business's own content, collects details and hands complex cases to a person.

**Facts to know:**
- Chatbots and virtual agents were used by 28.2% of AI-using businesses, and by 50.5% of AI-using firms with 100+ employees ([StatCan](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2026010-eng.htm)).
- A business is responsible for what its chatbot tells customers. In *Moffatt v. Air Canada* (2024 BCCRT 149, February 14, 2024), a BC tribunal held Air Canada liable for negligent misrepresentation after its chatbot gave wrong bereavement-fare information, and rejected the argument that the correct policy was elsewhere on the site ([Dentons summary](https://dww.com/articles/bc-tribunal-finds-air-canada-liable-for-inaccurate-advice-given-by-website-chatbot)).
- The OPC expects organizations to tell people when they are interacting with a generative AI tool ([OPC principles](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/technology/artificial-intelligence/gd_principles_ai/)).

**Recommendations:**
- Ground the bot only in approved content (policies, price lists, FAQs) and keep that content current.
- Do not let it quote prices, refunds or eligibility unless those come straight from a maintained source.
- Label it as AI, offer a human handoff, and review transcripts weekly at first.
- Bilingual sites: test French answers separately; quality can differ by language (**Opinion**).

## AI-powered inboxes, phone answering and lead qualification

**What it does:** sorts and drafts replies to email, summarizes threads, answers missed calls, asks qualifying questions (location, budget, timing, job type) and books appointments into a calendar or CRM.

**Evidence:**
- BDC profiled Winnipeg's OutreachGenius, whose AI voice agents answer calls for home-service contractors, book jobs into the CRM, and follow up by text, email or phone. Its founder estimates a caller who can't reach a business quickly has "almost 50% or more" chance of having already contacted a competitor, and says reviving dormant leads often produces 30% to 40% more revenue. These are the vendor's own figures ([BDC, July 13, 2026](https://www.bdc.ca/en/articles-tools/technology/artificial-intelligence/outreachgenius-using-ai-to-capture-missed-revenue-opportunities)).
- The same article reports about half of callers don't realize they are speaking to AI ([BDC](https://www.bdc.ca/en/articles-tools/technology/artificial-intelligence/outreachgenius-using-ai-to-capture-missed-revenue-opportunities)), which is why disclosure matters (see [ai-privacy-and-governance.md](ai-privacy-and-governance.md)).

**Recommendations:**
- Start with speed-to-lead: an instant acknowledgement plus qualification questions, then a person calls back.
- Keep the scoring rules visible and simple; a person should be able to see why a lead was marked "cold".
- Follow-up emails and texts are commercial electronic messages under CASL; see [federal-requirements.md](../06-canadian-regulations/federal-requirements.md) and [lead-generation.md](../01-getting-more-customers/lead-generation.md).

## AI sales assistants and AI in the CRM

**Useful tasks (Recommendation):**
- Summarizing calls and meetings into CRM notes and next steps.
- Drafting follow-up emails and proposals from a template.
- Flagging stalled deals and customers who have not ordered in a set period.
- Enriching records and cleaning duplicates before any scoring is trusted.

**What to know:**
- Major CRMs now sell AI agents with usage-based pricing (for example credits or per-resolution fees). Prices change often and were not verified on vendor pages for this file; check the vendor's Canadian pricing before budgeting (**Unverified**).
- AI output is only as good as the CRM data. BDC advises improving data quality at the source, for example using dropdowns instead of free text ([BDC, July 23, 2026](https://www.bdc.ca/en/articles-tools/technology/artificial-intelligence/how-to-prepare-your-company-data-for-ai)).

CRM selection and non-AI pipeline design are covered in [02-business-efficiency](../02-business-efficiency/pillar-overview.md).

## AI content production and marketing

**Good uses (Recommendation):** first drafts of blog posts, emails and social posts; repurposing one piece into several formats; product descriptions; translation drafts; image variations; ad copy variants for testing.

**Risks:**
- The Canadian Centre for Cyber Security lists misinformation, biased content and loss of intellectual property among generative AI risks ([ITSAP.00.041](https://www.cyber.gc.ca/en/guidance/generative-artificial-intelligence-ai-itsap00041)).
- The OPC names generating false or defamatory information about individuals, and deepfakes of identifiable people without consent, as "no-go zones" ([OPC principles](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/technology/artificial-intelligence/gd_principles_ai/)).
- Marketing automation use among AI-using businesses fell from 23.1% to 19.8% between Q2 2025 and Q2 2026 ([StatCan](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2026010-eng.htm)). The reason is not given.

**Recommendations:** a person edits and fact-checks every published piece; never publish AI-written reviews or testimonials; keep a house style guide the AI is given each time; for French-language content in Quebec-facing work, have a fluent reviewer.

SEO and AI search visibility are in [seo-and-local-search.md](../01-getting-more-customers/seo-and-local-search.md).

## AI reporting, analytics and internal knowledge bases

**Reporting and analytics:** data analytics is the top AI application among Canadian AI users (36.6%), and the top one for firms with 1 to 4 employees (32.5%) ([StatCan](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2026010-eng.htm)). Typical SME uses: asking plain-language questions of sales or job-costing spreadsheets, weekly summaries, anomaly flags (unusual refunds, slow-paying customers).

**Internal knowledge bases:** an assistant that answers staff questions from the business's own procedures, price lists, product specs and past proposals. Office suites now include this kind of feature; for example Google lists Gemini chat and "Gemini Notebook" for turning business files into audio overviews within Workspace Business Standard ([Google Workspace AI, CAD pricing page](https://workspace.google.com/intl/en_ca/solutions/ai/)).

**Recommendations:**
- Keep one source of truth per topic and archive old versions, or the assistant will quote outdated policies.
- Restrict access by role; an assistant connected to HR or payroll folders can surface them to anyone who asks.
- Check numbers before acting; the OPC expects reasonable steps to verify outputs used in decisions about people ([OPC principles](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/technology/artificial-intelligence/gd_principles_ai/)).

## Repetitive tasks suited to AI, and processes that should not be fully automated

| Usually suitable (with review) | Keep a person in charge |
|---|---|
| Sorting and drafting replies to routine emails | Hiring, firing, discipline and performance decisions |
| Meeting and call summaries | Credit, pricing exceptions, refunds and disputes |
| First drafts of marketing content | Anything a customer will rely on as advice (legal, tax, medical, safety) |
| Data entry from forms, invoices and receipts | Final quotes and contracts |
| FAQ answers from approved content | Complaints, bereavement and sensitive conversations |
| Lead qualification questions and booking | Decisions using health, financial or children's information |
| Report summaries and anomaly flags | Publishing statements about named people |

**Basis:** The OPC expects organizations to remain accountable when automated systems support a decision, to offer a way to challenge significant decisions and request human review, and to be cautious in sensitive contexts such as health care ([OPC principles](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/technology/artificial-intelligence/gd_principles_ai/)). Bill C-36, if passed, would require transparency about automated decision-making for significant decisions ([ISED, June 2026](https://www.canada.ca/en/innovation-science-economic-development/news/2026/06/government-of-canada-tables-new-legislation-to-protect-childrens-data-strengthen-privacy-and-build-trust-in-the-digital-economy.html)). The right-hand column is a **Recommendation**, not a legal list.

## Common misconceptions about AI in small business

| Misconception | What the evidence shows |
|---|---|
| "We don't use AI." | In BDC's 2024 survey, 27% of owners used AI tools without realizing it ([BDC](https://www.bdc.ca/en/about/mediaroom/news-releases/new-bdc-study-reveals-27-pourcent-of-canadian-entrepreneurs-don-t-know-they-re-using-artificial-intelligence-ai)). |
| "AI is only for tech companies." | 19.9% of businesses with 1 to 4 employees used AI in Q2 2026, close to the 19.2% average ([StatCan](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2026010-eng.htm)). |
| "Buying AI will raise productivity by itself." | A StatCan/ISED study found no statistically significant direct productivity effect once prior productivity and complementary capabilities were accounted for ([StatCan, 2026](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/36-28-0001/2026004/article/00002-eng.htm)). |
| "AI means layoffs." | 89.4% of AI-using businesses reported no employment change in 2025 ([StatCan](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2025011-eng.htm)). |
| "The chatbot said it, not us." | Rejected in *Moffatt v. Air Canada* ([Dentons](https://dww.com/articles/bc-tribunal-finds-air-canada-liable-for-inaccurate-advice-given-by-website-chatbot)). |
| "Canada has no AI rules, so anything goes." | No AI-specific federal act is in force, but PIPEDA applies to personal information in AI tools ([OPC principles](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/technology/artificial-intelligence/gd_principles_ai/)). |
| "Our data must be perfect first." | BDC says perfect data isn't required; start with the business problem and the data for one use case ([BDC](https://www.bdc.ca/en/articles-tools/technology/artificial-intelligence/how-to-prepare-your-company-data-for-ai)). |
