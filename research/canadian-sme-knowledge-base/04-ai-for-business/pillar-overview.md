# Using AI to Grow: Pillar Overview for Canadian SMEs

## The core question for this pillar

How can Canadian small and medium-sized businesses (SMEs), especially in Toronto and Ontario, use artificial intelligence (AI) to improve productivity, customer experience and profitability, without creating privacy, legal or quality problems?

## How many Canadian businesses use AI (2024 to 2026)

Statistics Canada's Canadian Survey on Business Conditions (CSBC) is the most reliable national measure. It asks whether a business used AI **to produce goods or deliver services**, which is narrower than "anyone on staff uses ChatGPT".

| Measure | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Businesses using AI to produce goods or deliver services, Q2 2024 | 6.1% | [StatCan, AI use Q2 2026](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2026010-eng.htm) |
| Same, Q2 2025 | 12.2% | same |
| Same, Q2 2026 (collected April 1 to May 6, 2026) | 19.2% | same |
| Businesses saying AI is not relevant to them, Q2 2026 | 40.0% | same |
| Businesses planning to use AI in the next 12 months, Q3 2026 | 25.2% (14.5% in Q3 2025) | [CSBC Q3 2026](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260831/dq260831a-eng.htm) |
| Businesses with no AI plans, Q3 2026 | 52.7% (66.7% in Q3 2025) | same |
| Workers who used generative AI at work in the past 12 months, March 2026 | 35.9% | [StatCan, generative AI among workers](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260730/dq260730b-eng.htm) |

**Why other numbers are much higher:** BDC's 2024 survey of 1,247 owners found 39% thought they used AI, but 66% said yes once shown a list of AI-powered tools; 27% were using AI without realizing it ([BDC, September 11, 2024](https://www.bdc.ca/en/about/mediaroom/news-releases/new-bdc-study-reveals-27-pourcent-of-canadian-entrepreneurs-don-t-know-they-re-using-artificial-intelligence-ai)). BDC's April 2026 LIFT release says 30% of SMEs used AI in 2025 ([BDC LIFT launch](https://www.bdc.ca/en/about/mediaroom/news-releases/bdc-launches-lift-getting-canadian-smes-off-the-ai-sidelines)). The figures measure different things; quote the source and definition with any number.

**Unverified:** Secondary reports say the CSBC's Q3 2026 cycle was its last. This was not confirmed on a Statistics Canada page; check before expecting future quarters.

## Who adopts AI: industry, size and location

From [StatCan's Q2 2026 analysis](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2026010-eng.htm):

| Group | AI use, Q2 2026 |
|---|---|
| Information and cultural industries | 42.3% |
| Finance and insurance | 40.4% |
| Professional, scientific and technical services | 32.4% |
| Construction | 9.2% |
| Wholesale trade | 7.9% |
| Agriculture, forestry, fishing and hunting | 4.5% |
| Urban businesses / rural businesses | 21.0% / 9.9% |
| 1 to 4 employees / 100+ employees | 19.9% / 27.8% |
| Businesses 3 to 10 years old / over 20 years old | 25.0% / 15.0% |

Workers show the same split: generative AI use at work was 65.6% in professional, scientific and technical services and 16.3% in accommodation and food services in March 2026 ([StatCan](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260730/dq260730b-eng.htm)).

**Implication (Opinion):** Toronto's large professional-services, finance and tech base sits in the highest-adoption groups, so competitors of a Toronto accounting, legal, marketing or engineering firm are more likely to be using AI than those of a rural contractor.

## What AI is used for, and what holds businesses back

Among AI-using businesses in Q2 2026, the most common applications were data analytics (36.6%), text analytics (34.5%), virtual agents or chatbots (28.2%), natural language processing (27.0%) and large language models (24.8%) ([StatCan](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2026010-eng.htm)). The smallest firms (1 to 4 employees) mostly used data analytics (32.5%) and text analytics (29.4%); firms with 100+ employees led with chatbots (50.5%).

Barriers named by businesses ([same source](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2026010-eng.htm)): cybersecurity or privacy concerns (13.4%) and cost (10.6%). Among Q3 2026 businesses with no plans, 79.1% said AI is not relevant, 10.8% cited privacy or security, and 9.9% lack of knowledge of AI's capabilities ([CSBC Q3 2026](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260831/dq260831a-eng.htm)).

BDC's 2024 study listed unfamiliarity with AI options, uncertainty about which tools to choose, data privacy and implementation cost, and found owners want "more concrete proof" before investing ([BDC](https://www.bdc.ca/en/about/mediaroom/news-releases/new-bdc-study-reveals-27-pourcent-of-canadian-entrepreneurs-don-t-know-they-re-using-artificial-intelligence-ai)).

## Does AI pay off? What the evidence says

- **Owner-reported benefits:** In BDC's 2024 study, 97% of SMEs using AI reported tangible benefits; 27% reported lower operating costs and 22% a reduced need to hire ([BDC](https://www.bdc.ca/en/about/mediaroom/news-releases/new-bdc-study-reveals-27-pourcent-of-canadian-entrepreneurs-don-t-know-they-re-using-artificial-intelligence-ai)). These are self-reported.
- **Measured productivity:** A Statistics Canada and ISED study found AI adopters had 16.8% higher labour productivity, but the gap fell to 5.1% and was **not statistically significant** after accounting for firms' prior productivity and complementary capabilities (data analytics, cloud, training). The authors conclude gains may depend on complementary investments and organizational change ([StatCan, April 22, 2026](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/36-28-0001/2026004/article/00002-eng.htm)).
- **Jobs:** In 2025, 89.4% of AI-using businesses reported no change in employment ([StatCan, Q3 2025 expected use](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2025011-eng.htm)). In Q2 2026, 44.4% of AI users changed training or staffing, mostly by training existing staff ([StatCan](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/pub/11-621-m/11-621-m2026010-eng.htm)).

**Recommendation:** Treat AI as a process change, not a software purchase. Pick one measurable task, set a baseline, and fix the data and workflow around it. See [ai-automation.md](ai-automation.md).

## Government direction: national strategy, financing and regulation in 2026

- **National AI Strategy "AI for All"** was announced June 4, 2026. Its six pillars include "Powering shared prosperity", which commits to driving AI adoption among SMEs, and "Protecting Canadians", which commits to modernizing privacy and online safety laws ([ISED, June 5, 2026](https://www.canada.ca/en/innovation-science-economic-development/news/2026/06/minister-solomon-highlights-canadas-national-artificial-intelligence.html)).
- **BDC LIFT**: $500 million to help more than 1,000 SMEs adopt AI, loans of $25,000 to $5 million, launched April 24, 2026 ([BDC](https://www.bdc.ca/en/about/mediaroom/news-releases/bdc-launches-lift-getting-canadian-smes-off-the-ai-sidelines)). Details in [ai-automation.md](ai-automation.md).
- **No federal AI-specific law is in force.** AIDA died with Bill C-27 on January 6, 2025. Bill C-36, tabled June 15, 2026, is a privacy bill, not an AI act. Details and status in [ai-privacy-and-governance.md](ai-privacy-and-governance.md).
