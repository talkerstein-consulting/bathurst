## The Canadian market context for customer acquisition

- **Almost everyone is online.** In 2022, 95% of Canadians aged 15 and older used the internet, and 84% had mobile data access for personal use ([Canadian Internet Use Survey, 2022](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/230720/dq230720b-eng.htm)).
- **Online buying is the norm.** Statistics Canada found 79% of internet users bought goods or services online in 2022, ranging from 59% of those 65 and older to 89% of those aged 35 to 44 ([Trends in online banking and shopping](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/240321/dq240321b-eng.htm)). In BDC's February 2026 consumer survey, 96% said they buy online at least occasionally ([Emerging trends and digital shopping behaviours among Canadian consumers](https://www.bdc.ca/globalassets/digizuite/62554-bdc-survey-emerging-trends-digital-shopping-behaviours-among-canadian-consumers.pdf)).
- **But most retail spending is still offline.** Retail e-commerce was 7.5% of total Canadian retail trade in July 2026 (seasonally adjusted) ([Retail trade, July 2026](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260924/dq260924a-eng.htm)). For most local businesses, online channels mainly drive discovery and enquiries, not the final sale.
- **Toronto is a crowded market.** The City of Toronto counted 74,560 business establishments and 1,623,710 employees in its 2025 survey ([Toronto Employment Survey 2025](https://www.toronto.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/97af-CityPlanning-Toronto-Employment-Survey-Two-Pager-Summary-2025.pdf)).
- **Costs are the main worry.** In the third quarter of 2026, 41.6% of Canadian businesses expected inflation to be an obstacle over the next three months, and only 14.5% expected sales to increase ([Canadian Survey on Business Conditions, third quarter 2026](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260831/dq260831a-eng.htm)). Marketing has to justify itself.

## How Canadian buyers find and evaluate local businesses

BDC's Consumer Compass survey (1,500 Canadian consumers, fieldwork February 12 to 17, 2026) is the most recent Canadian data on discovery ([Emerging trends and digital shopping behaviours among Canadian consumers](https://www.bdc.ca/globalassets/digizuite/62554-bdc-survey-emerging-trends-digital-shopping-behaviours-among-canadian-consumers.pdf)):

| Where consumers mainly learn about new products, services or brands (up to 3 answers) | Share |
|---|---|
| Friends or family | 54% |
| Online searches or advertisements | 50% |
| Social media post or advertisement | 41% |
| Online reviews or recommendations | 37% |
| Television | 26% |
| AI platform search | 8% |

In the same survey, regular habits when shopping for something new included comparing prices online (91%), searching for online reviews and recommendations (87%), visiting the company's website (87%) and asking a generative AI tool (38%). Among people who use reviews to discover offers, 76% use Google Reviews, 67% Amazon reviews and 41% Reddit.

What this means: word of mouth starts the journey, then buyers check search results, reviews and the website before contacting anyone. A business that is recommended but looks weak online loses the referral.

## What business owners are actually doing about it

- **Digital presence is common but shallow.** In CFIB's September 2025 survey of 2,478 members, 78% had a website, 59% used Facebook, 52% used Google Business Profile/Maps and 41% used Instagram. Only 33% of those with a website used it for direct sales, and 66% of digital users said lack of time was their greatest obstacle ([Small Business Digital Presence: Navigating Opportunities and Obstacles](https://www.cfib-fcei.ca/en/research-economic-analysis/sme-digital-presence)).
- **Owners advertise where buyers are less likely to look.** In BDC's 2026 survey, 74% of consumer-facing businesses promoted themselves on social media but only 31% used paid search ads, while 50% of consumers said they discover new offers through online search ([Emerging trends and digital shopping behaviours among Canadian consumers](https://www.bdc.ca/globalassets/digizuite/62554-bdc-survey-emerging-trends-digital-shopping-behaviours-among-canadian-consumers.pdf)).
- **Budget and measurement are the top barriers.** The same BDC survey found the most-cited obstacles to promotion were a limited marketing budget (46%), difficulty measuring the return on paid advertising (33%), paid advertising being too expensive (29%) and lack of time (28%).

## Main customer acquisition challenges for Canadian SMEs

1. **No time and no in-house marketer.** CFIB's 2025 data shows time is the biggest digital obstacle ([Small Business Digital Presence](https://www.cfib-fcei.ca/en/research-economic-analysis/sme-digital-presence)). BDC advisers say marketing is usually run by the owner or a part-time helper ([4 essential questions you need to ask yourself when marketing your business](https://www.bdc.ca/en/articles-tools/blog/4-essential-questions-you-need-to-ask-yourself-when-marketing-your-business)).
2. **Not knowing what works.** One in three businesses in BDC's 2026 survey found it hard to measure the return on paid advertising. Most of this traces back to missing call and form tracking. See [marketing-and-advertising.md](marketing-and-advertising.md).
3. **Weak local search visibility.** Only about half of CFIB respondents actively use Google Business Profile, even though Google says businesses with complete and accurate information are more likely to show up in local results ([Tips to improve your local ranking on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)).
4. **Leads are lost after they arrive.** Slow replies, voicemail and unclear websites waste paid and organic traffic. See [lead-generation.md](lead-generation.md).
5. **Rising costs squeeze budgets.** Cost pressure is the dominant obstacle in Statistics Canada's 2026 business conditions data ([Canadian Survey on Business Conditions, third quarter 2026](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260831/dq260831a-eng.htm)).

## Common misconceptions about getting customers

| Misconception | What the evidence says |
|---|---|
| "I can pay Google to rank higher on Maps." | Google states there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking ([Tips to improve your local ranking on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)). Paid ads are separate from organic rankings. |
| "SEO works in a few weeks." | Google says some changes take effect in a few hours and others can take several months ([SEO Starter Guide: The Basics](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)). |
| "Offering a discount for a Google review is fine." | Google prohibits incentives, including discounts and free goods or services, in exchange for reviews ([Prohibited & restricted content](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114)). |
| "Social media is where my customers find businesses." | Social is one channel. Online search and word of mouth are each cited by about half of Canadian consumers ([BDC Consumer Compass 2026](https://www.bdc.ca/globalassets/digizuite/62554-bdc-survey-emerging-trends-digital-shopping-behaviours-among-canadian-consumers.pdf)). |
| "AI search needs a special trick." | Google says there are no additional requirements or special optimizations to appear in AI Overviews or AI Mode ([AI Features and Your Website](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ai-features)). |
| "I can email anyone whose address I find." | Canada's anti-spam legislation (CASL, federal) requires consent and identification for commercial electronic messages ([Getting consent to send email](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email)). |

## Common marketing mistakes Canadian SMEs make

- **Spreading a small budget too thin.** BDC advisers warn that "dabbling in marketing with a few hundred dollars a month" won't get results ([4 essential questions you need to ask yourself when marketing your business](https://www.bdc.ca/en/articles-tools/blog/4-essential-questions-you-need-to-ask-yourself-when-marketing-your-business)).
- **Marketing without a plan or KPIs** (key performance indicators: the few numbers that show whether marketing is working). Same BDC source.
- **Asking only happy customers for reviews, or using review incentives.** Both break Google's policy ([Prohibited & restricted content](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114)). Staff reviews without disclosure can create liability under the federal Competition Act ([Online reviews posted by employees: businesses could be liable](https://www.canada.ca/en/competition-bureau/news/2024/01/online-reviews-posted-by-employees-businesses-could-be-liable.html)).
- **Keyword-stuffed business names and fake "city pages".** Google's guidelines don't allow unnecessary information in the business name ([Guidelines for representing your business on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177)), and pages built only to rank for different cities count as doorway abuse ([Spam policies for Google web search](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies)).
- **Running ads without conversion tracking.** See [marketing-and-advertising.md](marketing-and-advertising.md).
- **Buying email lists.** See the CASL note in [marketing-and-advertising.md](marketing-and-advertising.md).

## Emerging customer acquisition opportunities in 2026

- **AI-assisted discovery is small but growing.** 8% of Canadian consumers named AI platform search as a main discovery channel in February 2026, and 38% regularly ask a generative AI tool when shopping for something new. Only 6% of consumer-facing businesses promote themselves through AI search platforms ([BDC Consumer Compass 2026](https://www.bdc.ca/globalassets/digizuite/62554-bdc-survey-emerging-trends-digital-shopping-behaviours-among-canadian-consumers.pdf)). AI Overviews launched in Canada in October 2024 ([AI Overviews in Search are coming to Canada](https://blog.google/intl/en-ca/products/explore-get-answers/ai-overviews-in-search-are-coming-to-canada/)). See [seo-and-local-search.md](seo-and-local-search.md) and [../04-ai-for-business/](../04-ai-for-business/).
- **Paid search is underused.** BDC's 2026 data shows a gap between consumers using search (50%) and businesses advertising there (31%). B2B shows the same gap: 54% of businesses find suppliers through online search, but only 20% advertise on search engines.
- **Online booking is expected.** 57% of Canadian consumers regularly book personal-service appointments online and 52% make restaurant reservations online (BDC, 2026). See [../03-digital-presence/](../03-digital-presence/).
- **Buying Canadian.** In CIRA's March 2024 survey, 57% of Canadian internet users preferred to buy online from Canadian retailers when they had a choice, down from 71% in 2021 ([Trends in Internet Use and Attitudes](https://cira.ca/uploads/2024/07/2024-Internet-Trends-Factbook-FINAL-Report.pdf)). **Recommendation:** clearly show that you are a Canadian, Ontario or Toronto business where it is true.
- **AI adoption in operations.** 25.2% of Canadian businesses planned to use AI over the next 12 months in Q3 2026, up from 10.6% in Q3 2024 ([Canadian Survey on Business Conditions, third quarter 2026](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260831/dq260831a-eng.htm)). Uses like faster lead replies are covered in [../04-ai-for-business/](../04-ai-for-business/).
