# Lead Generation, Landing Pages, Website Conversion and Lead Follow-up for Canadian SMEs

A lead is a potential customer who has shown interest, for example by calling, submitting a form, booking or messaging. This file covers:

- where leads come from
- landing pages and sales funnels
- turning website visits into enquiries
- why many small business websites fail to produce leads
- responding to and following up leads
- the basics of acquiring eCommerce customers

Website build and platform choices are in [../03-digital-presence/](../03-digital-presence/).

## Where Canadian SMEs get leads from

**B2C (consumer) leads.** Canadian consumers mainly discover new offers through ([Emerging trends and digital shopping behaviours among Canadian consumers](https://www.bdc.ca/globalassets/digizuite/62554-bdc-survey-emerging-trends-digital-shopping-behaviours-among-canadian-consumers.pdf), BDC, February 2026):

- friends or family (54%)
- online search or ads (50%)
- social media (41%)
- online reviews (37%)

**B2B leads.** In the same study, businesses mainly find new suppliers through professional contacts (54%) and online search (54%), then trade shows (36%) and sales outreach (35%). BDC suggests:

- asking satisfied customers and partners for referrals
- staying visible in industry associations
- investing in search visibility "if budget allows"

**Lead sources by type of business.** **Recommendation:**

| Business type | Typical main lead sources |
|---|---|
| Trades and home services (GTA) | Google Maps and Business Profile, Google Ads or Local Services Ads, referrals, reviews |
| Clinics (dental, physio, chiropractic) | Google Maps, reviews, online booking, referrals from other practitioners, insurance directories |
| Accountants, lawyers, consultants | Referrals, Google search, LinkedIn, professional directories, seminars and content |
| Retail and restaurants | Google Maps, Instagram and TikTok, delivery and marketplace apps, foot traffic |
| B2B suppliers and manufacturers | Referrals, online search, trade shows, sales outreach, trade publications |

## Landing pages and sales funnels explained

A **landing page** is a web page built for one audience and one action, such as "book a furnace repair in Scarborough". A **sales funnel** is the series of steps from first contact to sale:

1. **Awareness:** the buyer sees you.
2. **Consideration:** the buyer reads reviews and visits your site.
3. **Conversion:** the buyer calls, books or fills in a form.
4. **Follow-up:** the buyer gets a quote or appointment, and the sale is closed.

**Why landing pages matter for ads.** Google Ads rates landing page experience as "how relevant and useful your landing page is to people who click your ad" ([About Quality Score for Search campaigns](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167118)).

**Recommendation: a landing page checklist for a Toronto service business**

- A headline that repeats the search intent and the service area (city or neighbourhoods).
- One primary action (call or book), visible on mobile without scrolling.
- Proof: Google rating, a few written reviews, photos of real work, licences or memberships where relevant.
- A short form (name, phone or email, what you need). Ask for an address only if you need it to quote.
- Price ranges or "starting from" pricing where you can give them.
- What happens next ("we reply within 1 business hour").
- A consent checkbox for marketing emails, unticked by default (CASL, federal; see [marketing-and-advertising.md](marketing-and-advertising.md)).

## How to turn more website visitors into enquiries

BDC's conversion advice ([Turn website visitors into paying customers: Easy tips to improve conversion](https://www.bdc.ca/en/articles-tools/marketing-sales-export/sales/internet-marketing-4-steps-improve-sales), updated 2025-03-10):

- Make calls to action "prominent, action-oriented, easy to find", and place them above the fold.
- A/B test (split test) two versions of a page to see which performs better.
- Make sure the site is responsive, loads quickly and works on every device.
- Show testimonials and case studies to build trust.
- Simplify forms. Don't ask for a full mailing address unless it's needed.

**What buyers check before contacting you.** In BDC's February 2026 survey, 87% of Canadian consumers regularly visit a company's website when shopping for something new. BDC advises that the website clearly show prices, features, reviews and comparisons ([BDC Consumer Compass 2026](https://www.bdc.ca/globalassets/digizuite/62554-bdc-survey-emerging-trends-digital-shopping-behaviours-among-canadian-consumers.pdf)).

**Online booking.** 57% of consumers regularly book personal-service appointments online and 52% make restaurant reservations online (same source). BDC calls digital ease "the price of entry".

Detailed conversion design (page speed, accessibility, UX patterns) is in [../03-digital-presence/conversion-and-user-experience.md](../03-digital-presence/conversion-and-user-experience.md).

## Why small business websites fail to generate leads

**Recommendation and opinion,** grounded in the cited sources where noted:

1. **The site can't be found.** It has no Google Business Profile link, thin service pages, or no pages for the services people actually search for. See [seo-and-local-search.md](seo-and-local-search.md).
2. **It doesn't answer buyer questions.** There are no prices or ranges, no service area, no process and no proof. Buyers compare prices, reviews and features online before contacting anyone (BDC, 2026, source above).
3. **The call to action is weak or hidden.** BDC recommends prominent, above-the-fold calls to action (source above).
4. **It doesn't work well on phones.** 84% of Canadians had mobile data access in 2022 ([Canadian Internet Use Survey, 2022](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/230720/dq230720b-eng.htm)), and local searches often happen on mobile.
5. **There is no trust evidence.** It lacks reviews, real photos, team names, licences or memberships, and a physical or service-area address.
6. **Forms are long or broken,** or they send enquiries to an unmonitored inbox.
7. **Nobody follows up fast,** so leads go to the competitor who called back first (see the next section).
8. **Nothing is measured.** No form or call tracking means no one knows which pages or channels produce leads. See [marketing-and-advertising.md](marketing-and-advertising.md).
9. **Websites are rarely used to sell.** CFIB's 2025 survey found only 33% of small businesses with a website use it for direct sales ([Small Business Digital Presence: Navigating Opportunities and Obstacles](https://www.cfib-fcei.ca/en/research-economic-analysis/sme-digital-presence)). Many sites are brochures, not lead tools.

## Lead response time and follow-up

**The research.** Harvard Business Review research by Oldroyd, McElheran and Elkington (March 2011) concluded that "most companies are not responding nearly fast enough" to online leads ([The Short Life of Online Sales Leads](https://hbr.org/2011/03/the-short-life-of-online-sales-leads)).

- **Flag:** This study is from 2011 and is US-based. Its detailed figures sit behind a paywall and were not verified here, so don't quote specific multipliers from it.
- No recent Canadian study on lead response time was found.

**Recommendation: a follow-up standard for Toronto service businesses**

- **Reply within one business hour,** even if only to confirm and book a call. Outside hours, send an instant automated reply saying when you'll respond.
- **Answer the phone or use a call-answering service** during business hours. Return missed calls the same day.
- **Use a CRM** (customer relationship management software) so every lead has an owner, a status and a next step.
- **Follow up quotes** 2, 7 and 14 days after sending, if there is no response.
- **Record the lead source** for every enquiry.
- **Check CASL before follow-up emails.** Under CASL, an inquiry gives implied consent for up to 6 months for commercial messages ([Getting consent to send email](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email)). Follow-ups after that need express consent or another basis. Rules: [../06-canadian-regulations/federal-requirements.md](../06-canadian-regulations/federal-requirements.md).

CRM and automation tools are in [../02-business-efficiency/](../02-business-efficiency/). AI-assisted replies and lead qualification are in [../04-ai-for-business/](../04-ai-for-business/).

## Qualifying leads so time goes to the right enquiries

Lead qualification means sorting enquiries by fit and readiness so staff focus on likely buyers. **Recommendation:**

- **Ask two or three qualifying questions in the form:** service needed, timeline, location or postal code, and budget range for larger jobs.
- **Define a "sales-qualified lead"** for your business, for example in the service area, a service you offer, and a timeline under 3 months.
- **Send poor-fit leads a helpful reply** or a referral elsewhere. It protects your reputation and reviews.
- **Price ranges on the website** reduce unqualified calls.
- **For B2B, keep a target list** of companies and use LinkedIn and referrals. BDC's 2026 survey found 55% of businesses promote through sales outreach, but only 35% find suppliers that way ([BDC Consumer Compass 2026](https://www.bdc.ca/globalassets/digizuite/62554-bdc-survey-emerging-trends-digital-shopping-behaviours-among-canadian-consumers.pdf)). Use outreach selectively for high-value prospects, as BDC suggests.

## eCommerce customer acquisition basics for Canadian sellers

**The Canadian e-commerce market:**

- In 2023, 32% of Canadian businesses received e-commerce orders, and e-commerce sales totalled $347 billion across the economy ([Survey of Digital Technology and Internet Use, 2023](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/240917/dq240917c-eng.htm)).
- Retail e-commerce was 7.5% of total retail trade in July 2026 ([Retail trade, July 2026](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260924/dq260924a-eng.htm)).

**Where Canadians buy online.** BDC's February 2026 survey found ([BDC Consumer Compass 2026](https://www.bdc.ca/globalassets/digizuite/62554-bdc-survey-emerging-trends-digital-shopping-behaviours-among-canadian-consumers.pdf)):

- 81% of online shoppers most often buy through marketplaces (Amazon, Etsy, eBay).
- 47% buy through retailer or brand websites.
- 18% buy through small or local business websites.

**What stops them buying from small businesses online** (same source):

- High shipping costs (54%)
- Limited product selection (35%)
- No online purchase option (29%)
- Untrustworthy-seeming businesses (19%)
- Websites that are not user-friendly (18%)

**What decides an online purchase:** shipping costs (37%), product availability (30%) and customer reviews (27%).

**Buying Canadian.** CIRA's March 2024 survey found 57% of Canadian internet users prefer to buy online from Canadian retailers when they have a choice ([Trends in Internet Use and Attitudes](https://cira.ca/uploads/2024/07/2024-Internet-Trends-Factbook-FINAL-Report.pdf)).

**Recommendation for Ontario online sellers:**

- Show shipping costs and free-shipping thresholds early.
- Offer local pickup in the GTA.
- Display reviews and clear return policies.
- Say plainly that you are Canadian.
- Test a marketplace alongside your own store.

Store build and platform choice (Shopify, WooCommerce) are covered in [../03-digital-presence/ecommerce.md](../03-digital-presence/ecommerce.md).

## Lead generation tactics to avoid

- **Buying email or phone lists for cold commercial messages.** CASL (federal) requires consent for commercial electronic messages ([Getting consent to send email](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email)). Telemarketing has separate federal rules (the National Do Not Call List); see [../06-canadian-regulations/federal-requirements.md](../06-canadian-regulations/federal-requirements.md).
- **Fake urgency or misleading claims** on landing pages. Deceptive marketing rules are federal (Competition Act) and provincial (Ontario consumer protection); see [../06-canadian-regulations/](../06-canadian-regulations/).
- **Paying for fake reviews or "lead lists" from unknown vendors** (see [customer-acquisition.md](customer-acquisition.md)).
- **Collecting more personal information than you need** in forms. Federal private-sector privacy law (PIPEDA) applies to commercial activity; see [../06-canadian-regulations/privacy-and-data-protection.md](../06-canadian-regulations/privacy-and-data-protection.md).
