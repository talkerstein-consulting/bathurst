# Marketing and Advertising for Canadian SMEs: Google Ads, Social Media, Email, Content and ROI

This file covers paid and owned marketing channels for Canadian small businesses:

- Google Ads (search engine marketing)
- Google Local Services Ads
- Social media, with Canadian usage data
- Email marketing, with a short CASL note
- Content marketing
- Budgets and measuring ROI (return on investment)

Budget benchmarks are in [customer-acquisition.md](customer-acquisition.md). Organic search is in [seo-and-local-search.md](seo-and-local-search.md).

## Where Canadian businesses advertise compared with where buyers look

BDC's 2026 Consumer Compass study compared where consumers discover offers with where businesses promote them ([Emerging trends and digital shopping behaviours among Canadian consumers](https://www.bdc.ca/globalassets/digizuite/62554-bdc-survey-emerging-trends-digital-shopping-behaviours-among-canadian-consumers.pdf), fieldwork February 2026):

| Channel | Consumers who discover new offers here | Consumer-facing businesses promoting here |
|---|---|---|
| Online search or search ads | 50% | 31% (paid search) |
| Social media | 41% | 74% |
| Television | 26% | 1% |
| In-app advertising | 12% | 6% |
| Streaming platforms | 10% | 3% |
| AI platform search | 8% | 6% |

BDC labels search, TV, in-app and streaming as opportunities: more consumers use them to discover offers than businesses use them to promote. Its B2B data shows the same pattern. 54% of businesses use online search to find suppliers, but only 20% advertise on search engines. 51% promote on social media, but only 24% use it to find suppliers.

**Opinion (BDC):** Rebalance towards high-impact channels such as paid search. Consider cutting social media spend if it isn't producing sales.

## How Google Ads works and what it costs

Google Ads is Google's pay-per-click advertising platform. According to Google ([Using Google Ads for online marketing](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6227565)):

- **Auction.** An ad auction runs every time someone searches.
- **Pay per click.** For search campaigns you are charged when someone clicks. You set a maximum cost-per-click bid and may pay less.
- **Daily budget.** You choose an average daily budget. On busy days Google can spend up to twice that amount.
- **Monthly cap.** Over a month you won't be charged more than your average daily budget times 30.4 (the average number of days in a month). Campaigns that pay for conversions are an exception.

**Quality Score** is a 1 to 10 diagnostic for each keyword. It is based on expected clickthrough rate, ad relevance and landing page experience. Google says it is "not an input in the ad auction" ([About Quality Score for Search campaigns](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6167118)). It is still a useful sign that ads and landing pages match what people searched for.

**Costs per click:**

- Google does not publish average Canadian costs per click.
- Keyword Planner shows search volume estimates and average cost estimates for your own keywords and location ([Use Keyword Planner](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7337243)). It requires a completed Google Ads account setup with billing information.
- **Estimate:** Toronto clicks for urgent trades, legal and health services are usually far more expensive than for retail products. Check Keyword Planner for your area instead of relying on published averages, which are mostly US data.

BDC quotes an adviser recommending at least $1,000 a month for search ads to be effective ([What is the average marketing budget for a small business?](https://www.bdc.ca/en/articles-tools/marketing-sales-export/marketing/what-average-marketing-budget-for-small-business)). That is opinion, not a Google minimum. Google's help page does not state a minimum spend.

## Google Local Services Ads in Canada

Local Services Ads (LSA) are Google ads where trades and home-service businesses "receive leads directly from potential customers" and "pay only for leads related to your business" ([Getting started with Local Services Ads](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841?hl=en&co=GENIE.CountryCode%3DCA), Canada version, accessed 2026-11-12).

Categories listed on the Canadian help page:

- Appliance repair, carpet cleaning, cleaning, electricians
- HVAC, junk removal, lawn care, locksmiths
- Movers, pest control, plumbers, roofers
- Tree services, water damage, window cleaning, window repair

Google says LSA is "available only for certain service categories". It asks you to check eligibility for your area.

**Recommendation:** For a Toronto plumber, HVAC or electrical contractor, test Local Services Ads alongside regular search ads.

- Compare cost per booked job, not cost per lead.
- Dispute leads that are clearly outside your service area or the wrong job type, if the dispute process allows.
- Your Google reviews matter here too.

## Search ads versus SEO: how to choose

| Factor | Google Ads (paid search) | SEO (organic search) |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | Ads can show as soon as the campaign is approved | Google says changes can take a few hours to several months ([SEO Starter Guide: The Basics](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide)) |
| Cost model | Pay per click, under a daily budget ([Using Google Ads for online marketing](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6227565)) | No per-click cost; costs are time, content and expertise |
| Effect on Maps ranking | None. Google says you can't pay for better local ranking ([Tips to improve your local ranking on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)) | Business Profile, reviews and website quality affect local results |
| When it stops | Traffic stops when spending stops | Rankings usually decay slowly (**Estimate**) |

**Recommendation:** Most Toronto service businesses benefit from both:

- Paid search for urgent, high-intent searches ("emergency plumber Etobicoke")
- SEO and Google Business Profile for steady, lower-cost leads over time

Start ads only after conversion tracking and a relevant landing page are ready (see [lead-generation.md](lead-generation.md)).

## Social media use in Canada by platform

**Which platforms Canadians use (CIRA, March 2024).** CIRA surveyed 2,000 Canadian internet users aged 18+ in March 2024 and asked which social media sites or messaging apps they ever use ([Trends in Internet Use and Attitudes](https://cira.ca/uploads/2024/07/2024-Internet-Trends-Factbook-FINAL-Report.pdf)):

| Platform | Share who ever use it (2024) | 2018 |
|---|---|---|
| Facebook | 61% | 77% |
| YouTube | 49% | not asked |
| Facebook Messenger | 49% | not asked |
| Instagram | 37% | 35% |
| WhatsApp | 35% | not asked |
| LinkedIn | 24% | 35% |
| X (Twitter) | 19% | 26% |
| Pinterest | 15% | not asked |
| TikTok | 13% | not asked (3% in 2020) |

**Which platforms Canadians use to discover businesses (BDC, February 2026).** Among Canadians who discover new products, services and brands through social media, the platforms used were ([BDC Consumer Compass 2026](https://www.bdc.ca/globalassets/digizuite/62554-bdc-survey-emerging-trends-digital-shopping-behaviours-among-canadian-consumers.pdf)):

- Instagram 66%
- Facebook 65%
- YouTube 62%
- TikTok 29%
- Reddit 20%
- LinkedIn 15%

BDC also found:

- 71% of these consumers find brand-created content most helpful, ahead of influencer content and paid ads.
- Instagram skews to ages 18 to 34, urban and higher-income users, and Ontario.
- Facebook skews to ages 45+ and Atlantic Canada.
- TikTok reaches younger adults, especially women.

**How the two sources differ.** CIRA measures any use among all adult internet users (2024). BDC measures discovery among social media discoverers (2026). The numbers answer different questions and should not be compared directly. Statistics Canada does not publish platform-by-platform adult usage in its recent releases reviewed for this file.

## What Canadian small businesses do on social media

CFIB's September 2025 survey of 2,478 members found ([Small Business Digital Presence: Navigating Opportunities and Obstacles](https://www.cfib-fcei.ca/en/research-economic-analysis/sme-digital-presence)):

- 59% actively use Facebook and 41% Instagram.
- LinkedIn has greater uptake among larger firms.
- TikTok and similar platforms are used by fewer than one in ten businesses.
- Lack of time was the biggest obstacle (66%), and content creation was another major concern.

BDC's guidance on measuring social media ([How to measure your social media success](https://www.bdc.ca/en/articles-tools/marketing-sales-export/marketing/social-media-marketing-how-measure-success), updated 2023-08-01):

- Track traffic, engagement, conversion rates of followers compared with other sources, and leads by channel.
- Use a dedicated landing page or promo code for social followers.
- BDC cautions that "correlation doesn't necessarily mean causality."

**Recommendation by business type:**

- **Instagram:** clinics, restaurants, salons, renovators (visual proof of work).
- **Facebook:** local community reach, especially audiences 45+.
- **LinkedIn:** accountants, consultants, engineering firms and B2B suppliers.
- **TikTok:** only if you can make authentic short video regularly and your buyers are younger adults.

## Email marketing for Canadian SMEs and the CASL application note

Email works best for retention: repeat bookings, seasonal reminders, newsletters to past clients and offers to existing customers.

**CASL application note (federal law; not legal advice).** Canada's anti-spam legislation (CASL) applies to commercial electronic messages, meaning messages that encourage participation in a commercial activity ([Canada's anti-spam legislation](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en)). ISED's guidance ([Getting consent to send email](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/canada-anti-spam-legislation/en/getting-consent-send-email), modified 2019-04-01) says:

- **Express consent:** someone has agreed, verbally or in writing, to receive your messages.
- **Implied consent:** applies, for example, with an existing business relationship. It may be valid for up to 2 years, or 6 months for inquiries or applications.
- **Identification:** messages must identify your business and anyone on whose behalf you send.
- **Unsubscribe:** every unsubscribe request must be actioned within 10 business days, at no cost to the recipient.

For full rules, penalties and record-keeping, see [../06-canadian-regulations/federal-requirements.md](../06-canadian-regulations/federal-requirements.md) and [../06-canadian-regulations/privacy-and-data-protection.md](../06-canadian-regulations/privacy-and-data-protection.md).

**Recommendation:**

- Never buy email lists.
- Add an unticked consent checkbox to quote and booking forms.
- Record when and how consent was given.
- Use an email platform that handles unsubscribes automatically.

## Content marketing that brings in local customers

Content marketing means publishing useful material (guides, FAQs, project pages, videos) that attracts and persuades buyers.

**What Google rewards.** Google asks whether content provides "original information, reporting, research, or analysis" and demonstrates first-hand expertise. It warns against content "primarily made to attract visits from search engines" and against extensive automation to produce content on many topics ([Creating helpful, reliable, people-first content](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content)).

**Conversational topics and AI search.** BDC's SEO article recommends conversational, specific topics. Its example is targeting "how to fix a leaking shingle roof in rainy climates" instead of "roof repair". It also defines GEO (generative engine optimization) as optimizing content for AI tools such as ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude ([SEO tips for small businesses](https://www.bdc.ca/en/articles-tools/marketing-sales-export/marketing/seo-small-businesses-10-ways-rank-higher), updated 2025-08-13).

**Short video.** HubSpot's 2026 survey of more than 1,500 marketers worldwide found short-form video was most often ranked among the top three formats for ROI (48.6%) ([2026 state of marketing: Data from 1,500+ global marketers](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/hubspot-blog-marketing-industry-trends-report), updated 2026-04-10). This is global vendor data, not Canadian SME data.

**Recommendation: content ideas with the best return for Toronto SMEs**

- **Service pages.** One page per core service, with real prices or price ranges where possible.
- **Project and case pages.** Neighbourhood-specific project pages with photos, for trades and renovators.
- **FAQ pages.** Answers to the questions sales staff hear every week.
- **Comparison pages.** For example "repair vs replace" or "incorporate vs sole proprietor (general information)".
- **Short videos** that show the team and the work.

## Measuring marketing ROI for a small business

**The measurement gap.**

- In BDC's 2026 survey, 33% of Canadian businesses said the return on paid advertising was hard to measure, the second most-cited obstacle after limited budget ([BDC Consumer Compass 2026](https://www.bdc.ca/globalassets/digizuite/62554-bdc-survey-emerging-trends-digital-shopping-behaviours-among-canadian-consumers.pdf)).
- Globally, HubSpot's 2026 survey found measuring ROI was marketers' top challenge (33%) ([2026 state of marketing](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/hubspot-blog-marketing-industry-trends-report)).

**What Google Ads can track.** Google Ads conversion tracking can record ([About conversion measurement](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/1722022)):

- website actions (purchases, form submissions)
- phone calls from ads and from your website
- app installs
- offline conversions you import, such as sales closed later

Google says this helps you "understand your return on investment (ROI)".

**Recommendation: a minimum measurement setup**

1. Track form submissions, calls (call tracking numbers) and bookings as conversions.
2. Ask every new customer "How did you hear about us?" and record it in your CRM.
3. Calculate cost per lead and cost per customer by channel every month.
4. Compare against the gross profit of a typical customer, ideally its lifetime value.

**Formula:** ROI = (gross profit from customers the channel won − channel cost) ÷ channel cost.

## Common advertising mistakes Canadian SMEs make

**Opinion and recommendation, based on the sources cited in this file:**

- **Sending ad traffic to the homepage** instead of a page that matches the search.
- **Running ads without conversion tracking**, then judging them on clicks.
- **Leaving broad targeting on** (location "interest" settings, broad keywords), which shows Toronto ads to people outside your service area. **Recommendation:** check location options and the search terms report.
- **Boosting social posts with no goal or offer.** BDC's data shows many businesses over-index on social compared with search.
- **Stopping and starting campaigns monthly**, which resets learning and makes ROI impossible to judge.
- **Paying an agency without access** to your own ad account and data. **Recommendation:** the business should own its Google Ads, Analytics and Business Profile accounts.
