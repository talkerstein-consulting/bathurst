# Local SEO, Google Business Profile and Technical Search Basics for Canadian SMEs

This file explains how a Toronto or Ontario business gets found in Google's local results and organic search: how local ranking works, setting up and verifying a Google Business Profile, the listing rules that get profiles suspended, and the website basics Google itself recommends. Review collection and Google's review policy are covered in [customer-acquisition.md](customer-acquisition.md). Search ads and Local Services Ads are in [marketing-and-advertising.md](marketing-and-advertising.md).

## How Canadians look for local businesses

How buyers discover and check local businesses (BDC 2026 survey data: friends and family first, then search, reviews and the company website) is summarized in [customer-acquisition.md](customer-acquisition.md) and [pillar-overview.md](pillar-overview.md). This file does not repeat those figures.

**Unverified:** no Canadian source was found that measures how many local searches include "near me" or a neighbourhood name (for example "plumber Leslieville"). Do not quote search volumes without a keyword tool export.

**Opinion:** for most Toronto service businesses the customer journey runs: recommendation or need, then a Google search, then the Map Pack, reviews and website, then a call or form. Local SEO decides whether the business is in that short list at all.

## How Google ranks businesses in local results

Google says local results are based mainly on three factors ([Tips to improve your local ranking on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)):

| Factor | What it means | What the owner can influence |
|---|---|---|
| Relevance | How well a profile matches the search | Complete, accurate categories, services and description |
| Distance | How far the business is from the searcher or the searched location | Very little; you cannot move closer to every customer |
| Prominence | How well known the business is | Reviews (count and rating), links from other sites, overall web presence |

Google's own tips: verify the business, keep information accurate and complete (address, hours including special hours, category, attributes), reply to reviews, and add photos and videos. Google states that there is no way to request or pay for a better local ranking.

**Recommendation:** be wary of any provider promising a guaranteed Map Pack position; Google says ranking cannot be bought.

## Setting up and verifying a Google Business Profile

Verification proves you are authorized to manage the listing ([Verify your business on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242)):

- Google decides which methods you can use, based on business type, public information, region and hours. You cannot pick freely.
- Possible methods: video recording (Google's recommended option where eligible), phone or text code, email, live video call during business hours, or a postcard with a 5-digit code (usually arrives within 14 days, expires after 30).
- Reviews of verification usually take up to 5 business days. Some profiles verify instantly, for example through Search Console.
- Automated phone systems cannot receive the code, so have a person answer the business line.

**Recommendation:** for a video verification, have signage, the business address, tools or equipment, and proof of management (keys, point-of-sale, business documents) ready to film in one continuous recording.

## Google Business Profile rules that get listings suspended

From Google's [Guidelines for representing your business on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3038177):

- **Name:** use your real-world name as shown on signage and stationery. No taglines, locations, phone numbers or keyword stuffing (for example "Smith Plumbing - Best Emergency Plumber Toronto").
- **Address:** a real, staffed location. P.O. boxes, virtual offices and rented mailboxes are not eligible. A co-working desk only qualifies with signage, staff and customer hours.
- **Service-area businesses** (trades, mobile services): one profile for the base, address hidden if customers are not served there, service area generally within about two hours' drive.
- **Hours:** customer-facing hours; appointment-only businesses should not list hours.
- **Categories:** as few and as specific as possible, completing "This business *is* a...".
- **One profile per location.** Practitioners should not create multiple profiles for different specialties.

**Recommendation:** home-based Toronto trades should hide their home address and set a service area of the neighbourhoods and municipalities actually served, rather than listing a rented mailbox.

## Website basics Google recommends for small businesses

Google's [SEO Starter Guide](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/seo-starter-guide) gives the essentials:

- **Get indexed:** check with a `site:yourdomain.ca` search and Google Search Console's URL Inspection tool. Google must be able to load the same CSS and JavaScript a browser does.
- **Titles and descriptions:** a unique, accurate `<title>` and short meta description for each page.
- **Descriptive URLs and links:** readable words in URLs; link text that says what the target page is about.
- **Images:** clear images near related text, with descriptive alt text.
- **Useful, current content:** organized with headings, original, and updated or removed when outdated.

What Google says **not** to worry about: the meta keywords tag (not used), keyword stuffing (against spam policies), keywords in the domain name (little effect), an ideal word count (none exists), and heading order.

**Recommendation for local firms:** one page per core service, and for multi-area businesses a page per real service area with genuinely different content (projects, staff, directions), not copied text with the city name swapped.

## LocalBusiness structured data

Structured data helps Google understand business details such as address, hours and phone ([Local business structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/local-business)):

- Required: `name` and `address`.
- Recommended: `geo`, `openingHoursSpecification`, `telephone` (with country and area code), `url`, `priceRange`.
- Use the most specific subtype (for example `Dentist`, `Plumber`).
- Review markup applies only to sites reviewing **other** businesses, so a business cannot mark up its own testimonials for stars.
- Google does not guarantee rich results; validate with the Rich Results Test.

**Recommendation:** keep the name, address and phone in the markup identical to the Google Business Profile and website footer.

## Local SEO priorities for a small Toronto business

**Recommendation** (Talkerstein practice, built on the Google guidance above), in order:

1. Claim, verify and fully complete the Google Business Profile with accurate categories, hours, service area and photos.
2. Set up a steady review request routine (see [customer-acquisition.md](customer-acquisition.md)) and reply to every review.
3. Make the website indexable, fast on mobile, with one clear page per service and click-to-call.
4. Add LocalBusiness structured data and connect Google Search Console.
5. Earn local mentions and links: suppliers, BIAs (business improvement areas), local associations, sponsorships.
6. Track calls, direction requests and form leads monthly, not rankings alone.

Weeks-to-months timelines for SEO are typical, but no source reviewed gives a reliable Canadian benchmark (**Estimate**).
