# Customer Acquisition, Referrals, Retention, Reviews and Marketing Budgets for Canadian SMEs

This file covers how Canadian small businesses win and keep customers: which acquisition channels customers actually use, referrals and loyalty, how buyers research local businesses, online reviews and reputation (including Google's review rules), and how to set a marketing budget. Paid advertising and social media detail is in [marketing-and-advertising.md](marketing-and-advertising.md). Search visibility is in [seo-and-local-search.md](seo-and-local-search.md).

## Which customer acquisition channels Canadian buyers actually use

BDC's Consumer Compass survey of 1,500 Canadian consumers (February 2026) found that 89% mainly discover new products, services and brands through digital channels and 40% through traditional media, with most people cross-checking more than two sources ([Emerging trends and digital shopping behaviours among Canadian consumers](https://www.bdc.ca/globalassets/digizuite/62554-bdc-survey-emerging-trends-digital-shopping-behaviours-among-canadian-consumers.pdf)). The single biggest source is friends or family (54%), followed by online search or ads (50%), social media (41%) and online reviews (37%).

The survey found generational differences:

- Younger (18 to 44), higher-income consumers rely mainly on digital channels.
- Consumers aged 45 and older, and those in Quebec, rely more on traditional media.
- BDC notes that TV and newspapers can still reach older consumers, often with less competition.

For B2B (business-to-business) sellers, the same BDC study surveyed about 400 business owners. 54% learn about new suppliers through professional contacts and 54% through online search. 74% use digital channels and 73% traditional channels, so BDC concludes that a single-channel approach "will likely result in missed opportunities."

**Recommendation:** Most Toronto service businesses should build acquisition in this order: (1) make referrals easy and visible, (2) be findable and credible on Google (Business Profile, reviews, website), (3) add paid search for high-intent searches, (4) use social media for proof and storytelling, not as the only lead source.

## Building a customer referral program that works

Word of mouth is the leading discovery channel in Canada (54% of consumers, BDC 2026, source above). BDC's guidance on referrals ([Boost your sales with business referrals](https://www.bdc.ca/en/articles-tools/marketing-sales-export/marketing/tips-how-win-clients-prospects-referrals), updated 2023-08-01):

- **Ask at the right time:** "The optimal time to ask is when your client is happiest", for example after positive feedback or a finished project.
- **Reward both sides:** for example, a gift card or discount for both the new customer and the client who referred them.
- **Keep it simple** so referring takes little effort.
- **Track the program** to see what works.

The same BDC article cites outside figures it did not produce: research it attributes to Texas Tech found 83% of satisfied customers are willing to give referrals but only 29% do, because they were never asked. It also cites a Heinz Marketing/Influitive report saying companies with referral programs report 71% higher conversion rates. **Unverified:** these figures come second-hand through BDC. The original studies were not reviewed, and their dates and methods are unknown.

**Rules to check:**

- A referral reward is fine. Referral emails or texts sent by your business can still be commercial electronic messages under CASL (federal). See the CASL note in [marketing-and-advertising.md](marketing-and-advertising.md).
- Regulated professions (for example health colleges and law societies in Ontario) may restrict referral fees. Check with your regulator.

## Customer loyalty programs and retention for small businesses

BDC's loyalty program guide sets out six steps ([6 easy steps to create a customer loyalty program](https://www.bdc.ca/en/articles-tools/blog/6-steps-create-customer-loyalty-program), date not stated):

1. Study your current customers.
2. Prepare your customer loyalty program.
3. Set goals and measure them with a CRM (customer relationship management software).
4. Set a budget.
5. Decide which customers to target.
6. Choose tactics that encourage loyalty.

The guide states that keeping an existing customer "costs up to five times less than winning a new one" and that 20% of customers typically produce 80% of revenue. BDC does not cite a study for either figure, so treat them as rules of thumb, not measured Canadian data.

**Recommendation: retention tactics that suit Toronto service businesses**

- **Reminders.** Service reminders by email or text (annual furnace tune-up, dental recall, tax-season check-in), sent with valid consent.
- **Follow-up after the job.** A thank-you message 1 to 3 days after the job, with a review request sent to every customer, not only happy ones.
- **Priority for repeat customers.** Repeat-customer pricing or priority booking instead of points programs, which need more admin.
- **One customer list.** Keep customers in a single CRM so the business knows when each was last served. See [../02-business-efficiency/](../02-business-efficiency/).

## How Canadian customers research a local business before buying

Statistics Canada data shows how normal online research and buying have become:

- 95% of Canadians 15+ used the internet in 2022 ([Canadian Internet Use Survey, 2022](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/230720/dq230720b-eng.htm)).
- 79% of internet users bought something online in 2022, and 89% of online shoppers used a credit card ([Trends in online banking and shopping](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/240321/dq240321b-eng.htm)).

BDC's 2026 survey shows the research steps before a purchase ([BDC Consumer Compass 2026](https://www.bdc.ca/globalassets/digizuite/62554-bdc-survey-emerging-trends-digital-shopping-behaviours-among-canadian-consumers.pdf)):

| Regular habit when shopping for something new | Share of Canadian consumers |
|---|---|
| Compare prices of alternatives online | 91% |
| Search for online reviews and recommendations | 87% |
| Visit the company's website | 87% |
| Compare features or warranties online | 86% |
| Go to a store to see the item | 72% |
| Ask a generative AI tool | 38% |

BDC's advice is to make sure your website clearly shows prices, features, reviews and comparisons. If customers can't buy on your site, it should say where and how they can.

**Estimate:** For local services where prices are not listed (renovation, legal, clinics), visitors will likely use reviews, photos of real work and clear service descriptions instead of price comparison. This is based on the habits above, not on a separate survey.

## Online reviews: why they matter to Canadian buyers

- 87% of Canadian consumers regularly search for online reviews and recommendations when shopping for something new, and reviews are among the main criteria for buying online, especially for younger consumers ([BDC Consumer Compass 2026](https://www.bdc.ca/globalassets/digizuite/62554-bdc-survey-emerging-trends-digital-shopping-behaviours-among-canadian-consumers.pdf)).
- Among consumers who use reviews to discover offers, 76% use Google Reviews, 67% Amazon reviews, 41% Reddit and 33% Consumer Reports (same source). Ontario consumers were more likely to use Reddit.
- Customer reviews were the third most important factor in deciding whether to buy online (27%), after shipping costs (37%) and product availability (30%) (same source).
- Google says "More reviews and positive ratings can help your business's local ranking" ([Tips to improve your local ranking on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091)).

BDC recommends asking customers for reviews, replying to them on key platforms such as Google and Amazon, and using reviews in your marketing and on your website.

## Google's review rules every business owner should know

From Google's Maps user-generated content policy ([Prohibited & restricted content](https://support.google.com/contributionpolicy/answer/7400114)):

- **No incentives.** Merchants may not offer payment, discounts, or free goods or services in exchange for posting any review.
- **No review gating.** Merchants may not discourage or prohibit negative reviews, or selectively ask only happy customers for positive reviews.
- **No pressure on site.** Businesses should not require or pressure customers to leave reviews while on the premises.
- **No fake engagement.** Paid reviews, and content posted from multiple accounts by or at the request of one person, are removed.
- **Conflicts of interest.** These include current or former employment, contractual relationships and family relationships.

From Google's guide to getting reviews ([Tips to get more reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)):

- You can ask customers to use a Google review link or scan a QR code.
- Replies should be professional, polite and short, without promotions. Apologize when appropriate, and keep private information out of replies.

Removing reviews ([Report inappropriate reviews on your Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4596773)):

- You can report policy-violating reviews through the Reviews Management Tool and file a one-time appeal.
- Google says not to report a review just because you disagree with it, and that it doesn't get involved in disputes between businesses and customers.

## Canadian legal risk from fake or employee reviews

This is a short application note, not legal advice.

The federal Competition Bureau warned in January 2024 that employees must disclose their connection to the business when reviewing it or its competitors. It also said "anyone who writes or permits writing reviews that give a false or misleading impression to consumers could be liable" under the Competition Act ([Online reviews posted by employees: businesses could be liable](https://www.canada.ca/en/competition-bureau/news/2024/01/online-reviews-posted-by-employees-businesses-could-be-liable.html)).

The Bureau recommends:

- training employees to disclose their connection
- running a compliance program with monitoring

Federal and Ontario rules on deceptive marketing are covered in [../06-canadian-regulations/federal-requirements.md](../06-canadian-regulations/federal-requirements.md) and [../06-canadian-regulations/ontario-requirements.md](../06-canadian-regulations/ontario-requirements.md).

**Recommendation:** Never buy reviews, never hire a "reputation" service that posts reviews, and don't let staff or family rate the business.

## Managing your online reputation: a practical routine

**Recommendation** (Talkerstein-style practice, built on the Google policies above):

1. **Ask every customer, the same way.** Send a review link by email or text after every completed job or visit. Asking everyone avoids "selective solicitation".
2. **Reply to every review within a few business days.** Google says a prompt response shows you value feedback ([Tips to get more reviews](https://support.google.com/business/answer/3474122)).
3. **Handle negative reviews in public, briefly.** Acknowledge the issue, apologize if appropriate, and move details to phone or email. Never disclose health, legal or financial details. Clinics and professional firms also have privacy duties; see [../06-canadian-regulations/privacy-and-data-protection.md](../06-canadian-regulations/privacy-and-data-protection.md).
4. **Report only genuine policy violations** (spam, conflicts of interest, off-topic content) through Google's tool.
5. **Reuse reviews** on your website and in proposals (BDC recommends this; source in the reviews section above). Get the reviewer's permission where needed.
6. **Watch Reddit and local groups** if you serve Toronto consumers. In BDC's 2026 data, Ontario consumers were more likely to use Reddit for reviews.

## How much Canadian small businesses spend on marketing

BDC's budget article ([What is the average marketing budget for a small business?](https://www.bdc.ca/en/articles-tools/marketing-sales-export/marketing/what-average-marketing-budget-for-small-business), updated 2023-08-01) gives two kinds of figures.

**Rules of thumb (BDC, not survey data):**

- B2B companies spend 2% to 5% of revenue on marketing.
- B2C (consumer) companies spend 5% to 10%, because they usually need more channels.

**Survey data (older, 2019):**

- A 2019 BDC survey of more than 1,400 Canadian businesses found small business marketing costs averaged just over $30,000 a year.
- Firms with 20 to 49 employees spent about twice that.
- Firms with 50+ employees tend to spend over $100,000.
- **Flag:** These figures are from 2019 and predate post-2021 inflation and advertising cost changes.

The same article also says search engine advertising needs "a minimum $1,000-a-month investment to be effective". This is a BDC-quoted adviser's view, not measured data.

BDC advisers also say agencies "generally have a commission of 20% of the ad spend" for managing digital ads ([4 essential questions you need to ask yourself when marketing your business](https://www.bdc.ca/en/articles-tools/blog/4-essential-questions-you-need-to-ask-yourself-when-marketing-your-business)). That is a general statement, not a Canadian price survey.

## How to allocate a small marketing budget

BDC advisers suggest a 70-20-10 split ([4 essential questions you need to ask yourself when marketing your business](https://www.bdc.ca/en/articles-tools/blog/4-essential-questions-you-need-to-ask-yourself-when-marketing-your-business)):

- 70% on proven revenue sources
- 20% on the "next best thing"
- 10% on testing new areas

**Recommendation: a worked example, not a benchmark.** A Toronto home-services firm with $800,000 in revenue applying BDC's B2C rule of thumb (5%) would have about $40,000 a year. A 70-20-10 split could look like this:

| Bucket | Example use | Example amount |
|---|---|---|
| 70% proven | Google Business Profile upkeep, review requests, website fixes, Google Ads on high-intent searches | $28,000 |
| 20% next best | Local Services Ads test, remarketing, referral rewards | $8,000 |
| 10% experiments | Short-form video, email newsletter, AI search content | $4,000 |

**How to use this:**

- Re-balance every quarter using cost per lead and cost per customer, not clicks or likes.
- BDC's 2026 survey found 33% of businesses struggle to measure paid advertising return ([BDC Consumer Compass 2026](https://www.bdc.ca/globalassets/digizuite/62554-bdc-survey-emerging-trends-digital-shopping-behaviours-among-canadian-consumers.pdf)). Set up tracking before spending (see [marketing-and-advertising.md](marketing-and-advertising.md)).
