## The Canadian context for digital presence

- **Most small businesses have a website, few sell through it.** CFIB's September 2025 survey (n=2,478) found 78% of small businesses have a website, rising from 70% of firms with 0 to 4 employees to 91% of firms with 50 or more. Only 33% of those with a website use it for direct sales ([Small Business Digital Presence: Navigating Opportunities and Obstacles](https://www.cfib-fcei.ca/en/research-economic-analysis/sme-digital-presence), CFIB, 2025-12-17).
- **Time, not technology, is the main barrier.** 66% of businesses already online named lack of time as their biggest digital challenge (same CFIB source).
- **E-commerce is steady, not booming.** 32% of Canadian businesses received e-commerce orders in 2023, about the same as 2021 (33%). E-commerce sales across the economy totalled $347 billion in 2023 ([Survey of Digital Technology and Internet Use, 2023](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/240917/dq240917c-eng.htm), Statistics Canada, 2024-09-17).
- **Retail is still mostly offline.** Retail e-commerce was $5.5 billion, or 7.5% of total retail trade, in July 2026 (seasonally adjusted) ([Retail trade, July 2026](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260924/dq260924a-eng.htm)).
- **Google rewards good page experience but does not guarantee rank for it.** Google recommends good Core Web Vitals "for success with Search" ([Core Web Vitals and Google Search results](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/core-web-vitals)), but says it will "always seek to show the most relevant content, even if the page experience is sub-par" ([Page experience](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/page-experience)).
- **Security is the owner's job.** The Canadian Centre for Cyber Security recommends MFA, HTTPS, patching and monitoring for every business website ([Security considerations for your website, ITSM.60.005](https://cyber.gc.ca/en/guidance/security-considerations-your-website-itsm60005)).

## Key findings for owners

**Recommendation** (based on the sources above):

1. Treat the website as a sales and service tool, not a brochure. Most Canadian small businesses already have one but don't use it to sell or book.
2. Fix speed, mobile and security before redesigning. These are measurable and Google publishes the targets.
3. Use existing platforms (Shopify, WordPress/WooCommerce, booking software) until a specific process doesn't fit them. Then consider custom development for that process only.
4. Budget for ongoing maintenance, not just the build.
