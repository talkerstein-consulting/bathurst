## Canadian e-commerce by the numbers

- **About a third of businesses sell online.** 32% of Canadian businesses received e-commerce orders in 2023, roughly unchanged from 2021 (33%) and up from 2019 (25%). E-commerce sales across the economy were $347 billion in 2023, not significantly different from 2021 ($398 billion) ([Survey of Digital Technology and Internet Use, 2023](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/240917/dq240917c-eng.htm), Statistics Canada, 2024-09-17).
- **Online retail is a small share of retail.** Retail e-commerce fell 3.5% to $5.5 billion in July 2026, or 7.5% of total retail trade, down from 7.7% in June (seasonally adjusted) ([Retail trade, July 2026](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260924/dq260924a-eng.htm)).
- **Small firms that sell online:** among small businesses with a website, 33% use it for direct sales. The most likely to sell online are arts and recreation (63%), retail (52%) and hospitality (49%). Shopify and WordPress are the most common platforms ([CFIB, Small Business Digital Presence](https://www.cfib-fcei.ca/en/research-economic-analysis/sme-digital-presence), 2025-12-17).

**Opinion:** For most Toronto retailers, the store supports in-person sales (click-and-collect, stock checks, gift cards) as much as it replaces them.

## Shopify vs WooCommerce for Canadian businesses

| Factor | Shopify | WooCommerce |
|---|---|---|
| Type | Hosted subscription platform | Free plugin for self-hosted WordPress |
| Hosting, updates, core security | Handled by Shopify | Owner's responsibility; owner backs up and updates WordPress, WooCommerce, plugins and theme ([WooCommerce self-service guide](https://woocommerce.com/document/woocommerce-self-service-guide/)) |
| Server needs | None | WordPress 6.9+, PHP 8.3+, MySQL 8.0+ or MariaDB 10.6+, 256 MB memory, HTTPS ([Server recommendations](https://woocommerce.com/document/server-requirements/)) |
| Subscription (CAD) | From $49/month monthly or $37/month annually (Basic) ([Shopify pricing, Canada](https://www.shopify.com/ca/pricing), accessed 2026-11-12) | No platform fee; hosting, paid extensions and developer time instead |
| Support scope | Platform support | Does not cover third-party plugins, themes or customizations (self-service guide above) |
| Flexibility | Apps and themes within Shopify's rules | Full code access |

**Recommendation:**

- **Choose Shopify** if you want low maintenance, sell mainly standard products, and don't have someone to manage updates.
- **Choose WooCommerce** if the store is part of a content-heavy WordPress site, you need unusual product or pricing logic, and you have a developer or care plan for updates and security.
- Either way, the business should own the store account, domain and payment account.

## Shopify plans and fees in Canada

From [Shopify's Canadian pricing page](https://www.shopify.com/ca/pricing) (accessed 2026-11-12, prices in CAD; check before quoting as prices change):

| Plan | Monthly billing | Billed annually (per month) | Online card rate with Shopify Payments |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic | $49 | $37 | 2.8% + 30¢ |
| Grow | $132 | $99 | 2.6% + 30¢ |
| Advanced | $517 | $389 | 2.4% + 30¢ |
| Plus | From $3,400 | Not listed | 2.15% + 30¢ |

The page also advertised a 3-day free trial then $1/month for 3 months on most plans. **Unverified:** the Plus price shown was inconsistent on the page, so confirm Plus pricing directly with Shopify.

**Shopify Payments in Canada:** Canada is a supported country. The business must be located in Canada and be an eligible business type, and may need to provide ID, proof of address and business registration ([Shopify Payments supported countries](https://help.shopify.com/en/manual/payments/shopify-payments/supported-countries)).

Add to the subscription: paid apps, a theme, a domain, and sales tax set-up (see [../06-canadian-regulations/federal-requirements.md](../06-canadian-regulations/federal-requirements.md) for GST/HST).

## What an online store costs beyond the platform

BDC notes that custom e-commerce solutions involve "a one-time fee of several thousand dollars", while template-based services charge a commission on each sale, and that ongoing costs include hosting, software, maintenance and cybersecurity ([How to keep track of costs to get your business online](https://www.bdc.ca/en/articles-tools/technology/create-website/how-keep-track-costs-get-business-online)).

**Estimate (Talkerstein view):** the biggest hidden costs are product data (photos, descriptions, variants, French translations), shipping and tax configuration, integrations with POS, inventory or accounting, and monthly app subscriptions.

## Securing an online store

From the [Get Cyber Safe Guide for Small Businesses](https://getcybersafe.gc.ca/en/resources/get-cyber-safe-guide-small-businesses) (modified 2024-05-21):

- When choosing an e-commerce platform, compare security features such as MFA, data encryption and real-time threat alerts.
- Keep the platform and software updated; outdated software can give attackers a way into an online store.
- Encrypt customer data wherever it is stored, including backups.
- Put point-of-sale systems behind a firewall and limit customer data access to authorized staff.

The Cyber Centre's website guidance adds MFA for all accounts, fewer plugins and tested backups ([ITSM.60.005](https://cyber.gc.ca/en/guidance/security-considerations-your-website-itsm60005)). Privacy obligations for customer data are in [../06-canadian-regulations/privacy-and-data-protection.md](../06-canadian-regulations/privacy-and-data-protection.md).

## When a business should (and should not) sell online

**Recommendation:**

- **Sell online** when products are standard, can ship or be picked up, and customers already ask to buy without calling.
- **Use "request a quote" or booking instead** when prices depend on site visits, custom specs or volume (contractors, manufacturers, B2B wholesale). A B2B ordering portal may fit better than a public store; see [custom-development.md](custom-development.md).
- **Start small:** a short catalogue of best sellers, gift cards or local pickup tests demand before a full catalogue.
- About one in ten businesses stay offline, and 55% of those say digital tools aren't relevant to their customers (CFIB, source above). That can be a valid choice.
