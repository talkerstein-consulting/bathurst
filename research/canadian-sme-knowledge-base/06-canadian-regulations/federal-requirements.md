# Federal Requirements for Canadian Small Businesses

This file covers the **federal** rules that apply to most small and medium-sized businesses (SMEs) in Canada: CRA registration, GST/HST, corporate income tax, payroll, record keeping, and federal advertising and pricing law. Ontario rules are in [ontario-requirements.md](ontario-requirements.md), privacy and anti-spam rules (PIPEDA, CASL) are in [privacy-and-data-protection.md](privacy-and-data-protection.md), and accessibility is in [digital-accessibility.md](digital-accessibility.md).

**This is general information, not legal or tax advice.** Rates, thresholds and deadlines change. Every figure below was read on the official page on 2026-11-12, with the page's own "date modified" noted. Confirm current figures with the CRA or an accountant before acting.

## How business rules are split between federal, provincial and municipal governments

In Canada, a small business usually answers to three levels of government at once. The split below is a simplified summary drawn from the sources in this folder.

| Level | Typical rules for an SME | Main bodies |
|---|---|---|
| Federal | GST/HST, corporate and personal income tax, payroll deductions (CPP, EI, income tax), federal incorporation, anti-spam (CASL), private-sector privacy (PIPEDA, in provinces without their own law), competition and advertising law, federally regulated industries | Canada Revenue Agency (CRA), Corporations Canada (ISED), CRTC, Competition Bureau, Office of the Privacy Commissioner (OPC) |
| Provincial (Ontario) | Business name registration and Ontario incorporation, employment standards, workplace safety insurance (WSIB), Employer Health Tax, consumer protection, accessibility (AODA), health-information privacy (PHIPA) | Government of Ontario, ServiceOntario, WSIB, Information and Privacy Commissioner of Ontario |
| Municipal (Toronto) | Business licences for listed business types, zoning, signs, building permits, public health inspections | City of Toronto (Municipal Licensing and Standards, Toronto Building, Toronto Public Health) |

**Recommendation:** When a rule is mentioned, always ask which level it comes from. A Toronto business can be fully compliant with CRA rules and still need a City licence, or vice versa.

## Getting a CRA business number and program accounts

A business number (BN) is the 9-digit number the CRA uses to identify a business. Tax accounts hang off it as "program accounts". The CRA's page [Program accounts you may need](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/business-registration/business-number-program-account/need-program-accounts.html) (page dated 2026-09-03) lists the main ones:

| Program account | Code | When you need it (CRA wording, summarized) |
|---|---|---|
| GST/HST | RT | If you need to collect GST/HST, for example if you sell goods or services |
| Payroll deductions | RP | If you are an employer or pay amounts related to employment |
| Corporation income tax | RC | If your business is incorporated |
| Import-export | RM | For importers, exporters or customs brokers (now administered by the Canada Border Services Agency) |
| Information returns | RZ | If you file information returns such as T5018, T5 or T5013 |

A program account number has three parts: the 9-digit BN, the 2-letter code and a 4-digit reference number (the CRA's example is `123456789 RT 0001`).

- **Registration is online by default.** The CRA announced that "starting November 3, 2025, registrations for new business number (BN) or CRA program accounts must be done online" ([Businesses: Go online to register for a business number or CRA program account](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/news/newsroom/tax-tips/tax-tips-2025/businesses-go-online-register-business-number-cra-program-account.html), published 2025-10-28). Exceptions that still need a phone call or paper form include reactivating a closed program account, a Canadian business owned only by non-residents, and a business owned by another business.
- **Provincial numbers are separate.** An Ontario business name registration or Ontario corporation gets an Ontario identifier as well; see [ontario-requirements.md](ontario-requirements.md).

## Federal or Ontario incorporation

A business can incorporate federally (through Corporations Canada) or provincially (in Ontario, through the Ontario Business Registry). [Corporations Canada](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/corporations-canada/en) (page dated 2026-08-28) offers guidance on "the difference between provincial and federal incorporation", choosing a numbered or word name, filing annual returns, and keeping a register of individuals with significant control (ISC), the people who own or control the corporation.

- The Ontario incorporation fee is listed in [ontario-requirements.md](ontario-requirements.md). Federal fees were not recorded for this knowledge base; check Corporations Canada's "Services, fees and processing times" page.
- **Unverified:** Commonly cited benefits of federal incorporation (name protection across Canada, operating in several provinces) are not stated on the Corporations Canada landing page that was reviewed. A federal corporation operating in Ontario also has provincial filing obligations.
- **Recommendation:** The choice between sole proprietorship, Ontario corporation and federal corporation affects tax, liability and paperwork. Decide it with an accountant or lawyer.

## When a business must register for GST/HST: the $30,000 small supplier rule

GST is the 5% federal goods and services tax; HST is the harmonized sales tax that combines GST with the provincial portion in participating provinces, including Ontario. The CRA's page [When to register for and start charging the GST/HST](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/when-register-charge.html) (page dated 2026-06-16) sets out the small supplier test for most businesses:

- If your taxable revenues **do not exceed $30,000** over four consecutive calendar quarters, you are a small supplier and do not have to register (you may register voluntarily).
- If you exceed $30,000 **in a single calendar quarter**, you must register, and your effective date of registration is no later than the day of the sale that made you exceed $30,000.
- Different thresholds apply to charities and public institutions ($250,000 gross revenue test and $50,000 taxable supplies test) and to other public service bodies ($50,000).

**Recommendation:** Owners often miss the single-quarter rule. A business that lands one large contract can cross the threshold mid-quarter and must start charging tax from that sale. Track revenue quarterly, not just yearly.

## GST/HST rates by province

The CRA's [GST/HST calculator (and rates)](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses/charge-collect-which-rate/calculator.html) (page dated 2025-04-01) lists these rates:

| Province or territory | GST/HST | Separate provincial sales tax (PST/QST) |
|---|---|---|
| Ontario | 13% HST | None |
| New Brunswick, Newfoundland and Labrador, Prince Edward Island | 15% HST | None |
| Nova Scotia | 14% HST (decreased from 15% on April 1, 2025) | None |
| Alberta, Northwest Territories, Nunavut, Yukon | 5% GST | 0% |
| British Columbia | 5% GST | 7% PST |
| Manitoba | 5% GST | 7% RST |
| Saskatchewan | 5% GST | 6% PST |
| Quebec | 5% GST | 9.975% QST |

- Which rate to charge generally depends on where the customer or the supply is located, not where the seller is based. An Ontario business selling to a customer in Alberta or Nova Scotia may charge a different rate than 13%. The CRA's place-of-supply rules decide this; confirm with an accountant for online and cross-province sales.
- Provincial sales taxes in British Columbia, Saskatchewan, Manitoba and Quebec are administered by those provinces, not the CRA, and may require separate registration.
- For the e-commerce angle, see [../03-digital-presence/ecommerce.md](../03-digital-presence/ecommerce.md).

## GST/HST reporting periods, filing deadlines and electronic filing

From the CRA guide [General Information for GST/HST Registrants](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/rc4022/general-information-gst-hst-registrants.html) (RC4022, Rev. 25):

| Annual taxable supplies | Assigned reporting period | Optional |
|---|---|---|
| $1,500,000 or less | Annual | Monthly or quarterly |
| More than $1,500,000 up to $6,000,000 | Quarterly | Monthly |
| More than $6,000,000 | Monthly | None |

- **Monthly and quarterly filers:** file and pay "no later than one month after the end of your reporting period".
- **Annual filers:** generally "no later than three months after the end of your fiscal year". Individuals with business income and a December 31 year-end must **pay by April 30** but have until **June 15** to file.
- **Electronic filing:** for reporting periods beginning on or after January 1, 2024, "electronic filing is now required for all GST/HST registrants", and the guide warns of penalties for not filing electronically.

## What a GST/HST invoice must show for input tax credits

An input tax credit (ITC) lets a registered business recover the GST/HST it paid on business purchases. To claim it, the business needs supporting documents. The [RC4022 guide](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/forms-publications/publications/rc4022/general-information-gst-hst-registrants.html) sets the information required by purchase size:

| Total sale | Information required |
|---|---|
| Under $100 | Supplier's name (or trading name), invoice date, total amount paid |
| $100 to $499.99 | All of the above, plus the GST/HST charged (or a statement that the price includes it), the status of each item when taxable and exempt items are mixed, and the supplier's GST/HST registration number |
| $500 or more | All of the above, plus the buyer's name, a brief description of the goods or services, and the payment terms |

**Recommendation:** Make sure your invoicing software (QuickBooks, Xero, FreshBooks, Wave and similar) prints your GST/HST number on every invoice, because business customers need it to claim their ITCs. See [../02-business-efficiency/business-automation.md](../02-business-efficiency/business-automation.md) for automating invoices.

## Federal and Ontario corporate income tax rates for small businesses

From the CRA page [Corporation tax rates](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/corporations/corporation-tax-rates.html) (page dated 2026-10-21):

| Rate | Federal | Ontario |
|---|---|---|
| Small business rate (Canadian-controlled private corporations claiming the small business deduction) | 9% net | 2.2% (reduced from 3.2%, effective July 1, 2026) |
| General rate | 15% net | 11.5% |
| Ontario business limit for the lower rate | Not stated on this page | $500,000 |

- A Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) is, broadly, a private corporation controlled by Canadian residents. Eligibility for the small business deduction depends on the corporation's income type, its associated corporations and its taxable capital.
- **Unverified:** The federal business limit is not stated on the CRA rates page that was reviewed. It is widely reported as $500,000; confirm with the CRA's T2 guide or an accountant.
- Sole proprietors and partners don't pay corporate tax; business income is taxed on their personal return.

## Payroll obligations when you hire your first employee

The CRA [Payroll](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/payroll.html) hub (page dated 2026-02-16) sets out what an employer must do:

1. Determine whether you need a payroll (RP) program account and open one.
2. Before paying an employee, collect their information, including the province of employment and completed TD1 forms ("get the completed TD1 forms before you pay an employee").
3. Calculate Canada Pension Plan (CPP) contributions, Employment Insurance (EI) premiums and income tax deductions on pay, benefits and special payments.
4. Remit the deductions to the CRA on time (including nil remittances where required).
5. File and distribute T4 and T4A slips and summaries.

- Contribution rates and maximums change every January; they were not recorded in this knowledge base. Use the CRA's Payroll Deductions Online Calculator or payroll software.
- Ontario adds its own employer obligations (WSIB, Employer Health Tax, employment standards); see [ontario-requirements.md](ontario-requirements.md).

## How long to keep business records and where to keep them

From the CRA page [Where to keep your records, for how long and how to request the permission to destroy them early](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/keeping-records/where-keep-your-records-long-request-permission-destroy-them-early.html) (page dated 2026-08-03):

- Keep all required records and supporting documents for **six years** from the end of the last tax year they relate to.
- "You must keep records at your place of business or your residence in Canada."
- "The CRA may give permission for you to keep your electronic records outside of Canada"; even then, records must be available to CRA officials in Canada in a readable electronic format.

**Recommendation:** This is one of the few concrete Canadian "data location" rules an SME meets. Before moving bookkeeping to a cloud service, check where records are stored and that you can produce them from Canada on request. Privacy-related data residency questions are covered in [privacy-and-data-protection.md](privacy-and-data-protection.md).

## Advertising and pricing rules under the federal Competition Act

The Competition Act is federal law enforced by the Competition Bureau. It applies to advertising by businesses of every size.

- **Drip pricing.** The Bureau's [Drip pricing](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/drip-pricing) page (dated 2026-08-24) describes it as offering a low price, then adding mandatory fees so the advertised price can't actually be paid. Its guidance: don't advertise a price consumers can't pay once fixed charges or fees are added. The only fixed charges that may be left out are those imposed by government, "such as sales tax". Variable charges can also raise concerns.
- **Environmental claims (greenwashing).** In June 2024, new sections of the Competition Act began requiring evidence for certain environmental claims ([Greenwashing: guidance for businesses](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/deceptive-marketing-practices/greenwashing-guidance-businesses), dated 2026-10-23). Claims about a product's environmental benefits must be based on adequate and proper testing, and claims about a business's activities on substantiation in line with internationally recognized methodology. The provisions came into effect June 20, 2024, and private parties could apply to bring deceptive marketing cases from June 20, 2025 ([Environmental claims and the Competition Act](https://competition-bureau.canada.ca/en/how-we-foster-competition/education-and-outreach/publications/environmental-claims-and-competition-act), archived version, modified 2026-10-23).

**Recommendation:** For a Toronto restaurant, salon, contractor or online store, the practical rule is to show the full price, including mandatory service, booking or "admin" fees, wherever the price appears (website, Google ad, menu, quote). Only sales tax may be added on top.

## Federally regulated businesses follow different employment, privacy and accessibility rules

A small group of industries is federally regulated: Ontario's employment standards guide gives airlines, banks, post offices and inter-provincial railways as examples ([Your guide to the Employment Standards Act](https://www.ontario.ca/document/your-guide-employment-standards-act-0), updated 2026-02-05). For these businesses:

- Ontario's Employment Standards Act does not apply; federal employment law does.
- PIPEDA covers their employees' personal information as well as customers' ([PIPEDA requirements in brief](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/privacy-laws-in-canada/the-personal-information-protection-and-electronic-documents-act-pipeda/pipeda_brief/)).
- The Accessible Canada Act applies instead of (or alongside) Ontario's AODA; see [digital-accessibility.md](digital-accessibility.md).

Most Toronto SMEs (retail, construction, professional services, clinics, restaurants) are provincially regulated.

## Canada's anti-spam law (CASL) and federal privacy law (PIPEDA)

Both are federal laws that affect everyday marketing and customer data. They are covered in detail in [privacy-and-data-protection.md](privacy-and-data-protection.md):

- **CASL:** consent, identification and unsubscribe requirements for commercial emails and texts; maximum penalties per violation of $1 million for individuals and $10 million for businesses ([Frequently Asked Questions about Canada's Anti-Spam Legislation](https://crtc.gc.ca/eng/com500/faq500.htm)).
- **PIPEDA:** the 10 fair information principles, breach reporting and record keeping.

## Where to find federal business programs and laws

- [Business and industry](https://www.canada.ca/en/services/business.html) (Government of Canada): the federal index of services for starting a business, permits, grants and financing.
- [Justice Laws Website](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/): the official consolidated text of federal Acts and regulations, including the Excise Tax Act (GST/HST), the Income Tax Act and CASL.
- [GST/HST for businesses](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/businesses/topics/gst-hst-businesses.html) (CRA, page dated 2026-07-29): the CRA hub for registering, charging, filing, remitting, rebates and confirming a supplier's GST/HST number.

## When a Canadian business owner should get professional advice

**Recommendation (not legal or tax advice):** Use an accountant (CPA) or lawyer for:

- choosing a business structure and deciding where to incorporate
- GST/HST on sales to other provinces, other countries or digital products, and voluntary registration below $30,000
- whether a corporation qualifies for the small business deduction
- payroll set-up for the first employee, contractors versus employees, and remittance schedules
- CRA audits, reviews or letters, and any request to destroy records early
- environmental, "Made in Canada" or comparative advertising claims
