# Ontario and Toronto Requirements for Small Businesses

This file covers **provincial** (Ontario) and **municipal** (City of Toronto) rules that most small businesses in Toronto and the GTA meet: registering a business, employment standards, WSIB, the Employer Health Tax, consumer protection and City licensing. Federal tax and payroll rules are in [federal-requirements.md](federal-requirements.md), privacy (including Ontario's PHIPA) is in [privacy-and-data-protection.md](privacy-and-data-protection.md), and Ontario's accessibility law (AODA) is in [digital-accessibility.md](digital-accessibility.md).

**This is general information, not legal advice.** Figures were read on the official pages on 2026-11-12; each page's "updated" date is given. Other GTA municipalities (Mississauga, Brampton, Vaughan, Markham and so on) have their own licensing by-laws, which this file does not cover.

## Registering a business in Ontario: business names, corporations and the BIN

From Ontario's [4. Register your business online](https://www.ontario.ca/page/business/start/register-your-business-online) page:

- "If your business has employees, facilities, or offices in Ontario, you must register using the Ontario Business Registry."
- Registration gives a 9-digit Ontario Business Identification Number (BIN) from ServiceOntario, which "is different than a Business Number (BN)" issued by the CRA. Most businesses end up with both.

Fees, from [Cost and time required to register, change or search for a business name, corporation or not-for-profit](https://www.ontario.ca/page/cost-time-required-to-register-change-search-for-business-name-corporation-not-for-profit) (updated 2026-09-29):

| Filing | Online | By mail |
|---|---|---|
| New business name registration (sole proprietorship or partnership) | $60, immediate | $60, about 15 business days |
| Business name renewal | $60, immediate | $60, about 15 business days |
| Incorporating an Ontario business corporation | $300, immediate | $300, about 15 business days |
| Annual return, Ontario corporation | $0 | $0 |

- Changes to a registration must be reported within 15 days ([Registering your business name](https://www.ontario.ca/page/registering-your-business-name), updated 2026-09-28).
- **Unverified:** How long a business name registration lasts (commonly stated as five years) was not stated on the pages reviewed. The renewal fee confirms registrations must be renewed.
- For federal incorporation and the CRA business number, see [federal-requirements.md](federal-requirements.md).

## Ontario minimum wage rates (October 1, 2026 to September 30, 2027)

From the Ontario [Minimum wage](https://www.ontario.ca/document/your-guide-employment-standards-act-0/minimum-wage) page of the Employment Standards Act (ESA) guide (updated 2026-09-28):

| Rate | October 1, 2026 to September 30, 2027 | October 1, 2025 to September 30, 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| General minimum wage | $17.95 per hour | $17.60 per hour |
| Student minimum wage | $16.90 per hour | $16.60 per hour |
| Homeworkers minimum wage | $19.70 per hour | $19.35 per hour |

Ontario's rates change every October 1. Federally regulated employers follow the federal minimum wage instead (see [federal-requirements.md](federal-requirements.md)).

## Who Ontario's Employment Standards Act covers

From [Your guide to the Employment Standards Act](https://www.ontario.ca/document/your-guide-employment-standards-act-0) (updated 2026-02-05): "Most employees and employers in Ontario are covered by the ESA." It does not apply to sectors under federal employment law, such as airlines, banks, post offices and inter-provincial railways, and has other narrow exclusions. The guide covers minimum wage, hours of work, overtime, public holidays, vacation, tips, leaves, termination and severance, record keeping and non-compete agreements.

## Vacation and public holidays for Ontario employees

- **Vacation time:** employees with less than five years of employment get two weeks; five or more years, three weeks ([Vacation](https://www.ontario.ca/document/your-guide-employment-standards-act-0/vacation), updated 2026-02-05).
- **Vacation pay:** at least 4% of gross wages (under five years) or at least 6% (five or more years).
- **Public holidays:** Ontario has nine: New Year's Day, Family Day, Good Friday, Victoria Day, Canada Day, Labour Day, Thanksgiving Day, Christmas Day and Boxing Day ([Public holidays](https://www.ontario.ca/document/your-guide-employment-standards-act-0/public-holidays), updated 2024-07-08). Civic Holiday (August) and Easter Monday are not ESA public holidays.

## Written policies required for Ontario employers with 25 or more employees

Ontario employers with **25 or more employees on January 1** of a year must have certain written policies in place **before March 1** of that year. Employees are counted individually (part-time and casual staff count as one each), and staff at all Ontario locations are combined.

- **Electronic monitoring policy:** must state whether the employer electronically monitors employees, and if so how, in what circumstances and for what purposes the information may be used, plus the date prepared and dates of changes. Copies must go to employees within 30 calendar days ([Written policy on electronic monitoring of employees](https://www.ontario.ca/document/your-guide-employment-standards-act-0/written-policy-electronic-monitoring-employees), updated 2024-07-08).
- **Disconnecting from work policy:** the ESA guide has a parallel section, [Written policy on disconnecting from work](https://www.ontario.ca/document/your-guide-employment-standards-act-0/written-policy-disconnecting-from-work); it was not reviewed in detail for this knowledge base.

**Recommendation:** If you use time-tracking, GPS, email monitoring or AI tools that monitor staff activity, the electronic monitoring policy needs to describe them. This connects to [../04-ai-for-business/ai-privacy-and-governance.md](../04-ai-for-business/ai-privacy-and-governance.md).

## Job posting rules in Ontario from January 1, 2026 (pay ranges, AI disclosure)

From [Requirements related to publicly advertised job postings](https://www.ontario.ca/document/your-guide-employment-standards-act-0/requirements-related-publicly-advertised-job) (updated 2026-02-24). The rules apply as of January 1, 2026, and **do not apply to employers with fewer than 25 employees** on the day the posting goes up.

For employers with 25 or more employees, a publicly advertised job posting must:

- **Include expected compensation or a range.** A range can be no wider than "an amount equivalent to $50,000 per year or less". This does not apply where pay is more than $200,000 a year.
- **Disclose AI use** if artificial intelligence is used to screen, assess or select applicants. The ESA defines AI as "a machine-based system that, for explicit or implicit objectives, infers from the input it receives" how to generate outputs.
- **State whether the posting is for an existing vacancy.**
- **Not require Canadian experience** in the posting or application form (professional licensing requirements are excluded).

Employers must also tell interviewed applicants whether a hiring decision has been made, generally within 45 days after the last interview, and keep postings and related records for three years.

From [Recent changes](https://www.ontario.ca/document/your-guide-employment-standards-act-0/recent-changes) (updated 2026-02-05):

| Change | Effective |
|---|---|
| Employees can choose the account wages are deposited into; employers can't restrict it to one bank | June 21, 2024 |
| Vacation pay on each cheque requires a written or electronic agreement | June 21, 2024 |

## Registering with the WSIB after hiring your first worker

The Workplace Safety and Insurance Board (WSIB) is Ontario's workplace injury insurance system. From [How to register your business](https://www.wsib.ca/en/businesses/registration-and-coverage/how-register-your-business) (updated 2026-07-30):

- "Most Ontario businesses that have employees (including family members and sub-contractors) must register with us."
- "You have 10 calendar days to register with us from the day you hire your first employee."
- Independent operators outside construction don't need to register. Construction businesses' obligations "may differ"; the WSIB has separate rules on compulsory coverage in construction (see [../05-industry-research/construction.md](../05-industry-research/construction.md)).

## Ontario Employer Health Tax (EHT) and the $1 million exemption

The Employer Health Tax is an Ontario payroll tax. From [Employer Health Tax (EHT)](https://www.ontario.ca/document/employer-health-tax-eht) (updated 2026-09-18):

- "The exemption amount is currently $1 million." Eligible employers pay EHT only on Ontario payroll above that amount.
- To claim the exemption, total Ontario payroll for the year (including associated employers) must be **less than $5 million**, or the employer must be a registered charity.
- Rates rise with total Ontario payroll, from 0.98% (payroll up to $200,000) to 1.95% (payroll over $400,000), applied to taxable payroll after the exemption.

**Estimate:** In practice, a small business with Ontario payroll under $1 million owes no EHT. This follows from the exemption; confirm registration and filing rules with an accountant.

## Ontario consumer protection rules for businesses selling to consumers

The **Consumer Protection Act, 2002** is still the law in force. The new **Consumer Protection Act, 2023** was passed but, as of the e-Laws currency date of November 9, 2026, "THIS ACT IS NOT YET IN FORCE. It comes into force on a day to be named by proclamation" ([Consumer Protection Act, 2023, S.O. 2023, c. 23, Sched. 1](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/23c23)). Ontario's [Business Guide to Consumer Protection](https://www.ontario.ca/document/business-guide-consumer-protection) (updated 2024-10-04) confirms the 2002 Act remains in effect until then, and that "any uncertainty in a consumer agreement must be interpreted in favour of the consumer".

Consumer rights businesses most often meet ([Your rights under the Consumer Protection Act](https://www.ontario.ca/page/your-rights-under-consumer-protection-act), updated 2025-04-01):

- **Cooling-off periods** apply to door-to-door purchases, prepaid gym memberships, newly built condos, payday loans and timeshares. For most contracts cancelled in a cooling-off period, the business has 15 days to refund.
- **Unsolicited goods:** consumers can't be charged for items or services they didn't request.
- **Unfair practices** are prohibited.

**Gift cards** ([Buying or using gift cards](https://www.ontario.ca/page/buying-or-using-gift-cards), updated 2026-08-14):

- "The Consumer Protection Act bans most retail business gift cards from having an expiry date." Exceptions: cards for one specific service (such as a spa treatment) and cards issued for charitable purposes.
- Most businesses can't charge activation, service or dormancy fees on retail gift cards; fees for customizing or replacing a lost card are allowed. Shopping mall cards follow separate rules.

**Recommendation:** Online stores, renovation contractors, gyms and anyone selling to consumers in their homes should have their contracts reviewed by a lawyer, because the 2023 Act will replace the current rules when proclaimed. Advertising price rules (drip pricing) are federal; see [federal-requirements.md](federal-requirements.md).

## City of Toronto business licences, zoning and permits

From the City of Toronto's [Business Regulations, Licences & Permits](https://www.toronto.ca/business-economy/new-businesses-startups/business-regulations/) page:

- "All businesses in Toronto need to secure the proper licences, permits and inspections prior to opening or operating."
- Many business types need a municipal operating licence (the City lists examples such as restaurants, car dealerships and parking lots). For some, such as construction trades, it "depends on services offered".
- Zoning approval and sign permits appear as requirements for nearly every business type. Zoning questions for a specific property go to a Toronto Building Customer Service counter.
- Municipal Licensing and Standards requires criminal record and judicial matters checks for licence applications and renewals (in place since November 1, 2018).

The licensing by-law is Toronto Municipal Code Chapter 545. The City's [Business & Economy](https://www.toronto.ca/business-economy/) portal links to licensing, permits and business support.

**Recommendation:** Before signing a commercial lease in Toronto, confirm the property's zoning permits your use and check which licences and inspections (for example Toronto Public Health for food premises) apply. Home-based businesses should check zoning too.

## Ontario laws and where to read them

- [e-Laws](https://www.ontario.ca/laws) (Government of Ontario): the official consolidated text of Ontario statutes and regulations, with a "currency date" showing how up to date each consolidation is. Use it to check whether a law (like the Consumer Protection Act, 2023) is in force.
- [Consumer Protection Ontario](https://www.ontario.ca/page/consumer-protection-ontario) (updated 2026-05-15): consumer rights topics, complaints, the Consumer Beware List, and sector guides for businesses such as home renovation, door-to-door sales and auto repair.
- Ontario's corporate income tax rates (small business rate 2.2% from July 1, 2026) are listed in [federal-requirements.md](federal-requirements.md), because the CRA collects Ontario corporate tax.

## When an Ontario business owner should get professional advice

**Recommendation (not legal advice):** Speak to an employment lawyer or HR professional before terminating an employee, drafting contracts (including non-compete clauses), setting up monitoring, or crossing the 25-employee mark (written policies and job posting rules). Speak to a lawyer about consumer contracts and to an accountant about EHT and WSIB premiums. Check City of Toronto zoning and licensing before leasing space.
